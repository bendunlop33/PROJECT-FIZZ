# Range Architecture — Sketch

**Author:** CSO · **Date:** 2026-09-22
**Status: a SKETCH for the user to react to, not a recommendation and not a decision pack.** `proposition-architect` has not run and Gate 2 has not been reached. Every number below is transcribed from `research/ingredient-benchmark.csv` (97 products) or the parsed GB NHC register; the *choices* are OPINION.

---

## 1. The strategic frame, corrected

The user's framing on 2026-09-22: **this is not about inventing something nobody has. It is about cementing a niche — functional, vitamin-led, flavoured cubes for your water bottle — with enough range that one product is not trying to do everything.**

That is a better frame than the one the research has been chasing, and the evidence supports it. Three findings converge:

1. **Waterdrop defined the cube and owns the flavour-led end of it.** 2.0–2.3 g, twelve flavours, and a vitamin load deliberately held at 15–50% NRV (§3 of `cube-category-map.md`).
2. **The functional end of the cube is unoccupied.** Phizz Daily Immune+ has the function — the complete authorised immune set and the complete iron system — but it is a tablet in a tube, pharmacy-coded, sold through Holland & Barrett, and does not market on the interaction at all. Its own site publishes no panel.
3. **Nobody has copied the cube form factor in four years** (`cube-category-map.md` §6). Whatever that means, it is not a crowded niche.

**So the niche is: the cube, done functionally.** Owning it is a brand and consistency problem, not an invention problem. The question stops being "what can we make that nobody has" and becomes "what would the definitive version of this look like".

---

## 2. What a range has to satisfy

Six constraints, all already evidenced. Any architecture that breaks one is wrong.

| # | Constraint | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **2.0–2.3 g per cube.** Bulky minerals at full NRV are impossible; µg and low-mg nutrients are nearly free | A-066 |
| 2 | **Taste degrades as dose climbs.** Observed band: light 4.7 → 25–50% NRV 4.1 → 375% 3.5 → 595%+ 2.8. **Design inside 50–100% NRV** | A-076 |
| 3 | **Flavour intensity per cube at 500 ml is the category's biggest unfixed complaint**, five years old, with a two-cube workaround that doubles the customer's cost | `review-mining.md` |
| 4 | **Every authorised combination claim needs only 15% NRV** — so combinations are free in mass and sit below the taste ceiling | A-009, §7 of `category-benchmarks.md` |
| 5 | **Benefit-led names may themselves be health claims.** Waterdrop's surviving names are composition statements | A-074 |
| 6 | **Under D2C-only, each SKU must not be a separate acquisition funnel.** One brand, one format, one occasion; the range is a choice at checkout | A-028 |

## 3. Three SKUs, each with a job the market demonstrably leaves open

Each does one thing, passes its own category's benchmark (`category-benchmarks.md`), and fills a hole the 97-product benchmark actually shows.

### SKU 1 — the everyday cube · *"Vitamins + Minerals"*

**The job:** the daily multivitamin you drink instead of swallow.

**The hole it fills, and it is the widest one in the dataset.** Of 71 panelled products, only **2** carry all five coverage markers — vitamin D, riboflavin, folate, iodine, selenium. **38 carry none of them.** And **iodine is the rarest nutrient in the entire UK market: 3 of 71**, of which one is a children's product and one is a swallowed tablet. **No adult everyday-vitamin drink in the UK declares iodine.** No Waterdrop product declares vitamin D, iodine or selenium at all.

**Spec direction:** the five markers, plus C and the B-complex, everything in the 50–100% NRV band. All five markers are µg-to-low-mg, so the mass cost is trivial — the reason nobody carries them is not that they cannot.

**Why it holds:** this is a coverage claim, not a dose claim. Waterdrop can raise a dose in an afternoon; adding iodine and selenium to a range it has never carried is a reformulation across twelve SKUs.

### SKU 2 — the hydration cube · *"Electrolytes + Vitamins"*

**The job:** actually rehydrate, which the category leader does not.

**The hole:** **16 of 41 hydration products declare no sodium at all.** Waterdrop Microlyte Berry declares 0.16 g salt — roughly **63 mg sodium** — against a category range of 116–1,034 mg, and declares no potassium despite potassium chloride being its second ingredient.

**Spec direction:** a real, declared sodium and potassium figure. Sodium and potassium salts are light and cheap; this is not a mass problem. Out-specifying the format's leader on the format's own benchmark is available for pennies.

### SKU 3 — the antioxidant cube · *"Antioxidant Complex"*

**The job:** the range's functional flagship, and the one genuinely unbuilt thing in the dataset.

**The hole:** the authorised *"contributes to the protection of cells from oxidative stress"* claim belongs to exactly seven nutrients — **copper, manganese, riboflavin, selenium, vitamin C, vitamin E, zinc**. **No UK drink completes the set.** The best are three at 6/7, each missing a different one: High5 (copper), Phizz 3-in-1 (selenium), Phizz Immune+ (manganese). Only Centrum, a swallowed tablet, completes it.

**Spec direction:** all seven. Every one is µg-to-low-mg, so the complete set fits a 2.0 g cube comfortably, and every one needs only 15% NRV — well below the taste ceiling.

**Why this is the flagship:** it is the only place where the brand can say *seven nutrients, all seven of the ones the law recognises, nobody else has done it* — and have that be literally true and legally sayable.

## 4. Why three, and why these three

- **Three distinct jobs, no overlap.** Everyday coverage · hydration · antioxidant. A customer can articulate which one they want without help, which is the test the Vitamin Well range fails (Reload and Recover are formulation-identical).
- **They share one funnel.** Same brand, same cube, same occasion — a cube in your water bottle. The range is a choice at checkout, not three products to market. That is the only shape A-028 permits.
- **Each passes its own benchmark**, so none is a product wearing a badge it has not earned — the failure mode found in 16 of 41 hydration products, and in Microlyte specifically.
- **It respects the logged 3-product cap** without pretending the cap was arbitrary.

**Deliberately excluded, with reasons:** *energy* (needs caffeine to mean anything, and caffeine is a decided no — and carries no authorised claim regardless, A-052); *focus* (one authorised sentence, no felt mechanism, A-052); *sleep, calm, stress* (no authorised GB claim exists at all — the trap in `opportunity-areas.md`); *immune* (Phizz Daily Immune+ already holds the complete set, so it is the one territory that is genuinely taken).

## 5. What must be decided before this is worth costing

1. **Naming (A-074).** "Daily", "Restore", "Defend" are benefit names and may be implied health claims. Waterdrop's surviving names are all composition statements — *Hydration + Vitamins*, *+ Electrolytes*, *+ Energy* — which is a strong hint. **Default to composition naming until the food-law opinion lands.**
2. **Flavour intensity.** The whole proposition rests on tasting good, and the category's five-year-old unfixed complaint is that one cube does not flavour 500 ml. **Fixing that is the product, not a detail.**
3. **The taste test.** Nothing in this project measures taste. Six products, thirty people, one afternoon — it would settle both the flavour-intensity target and whether "designed to work together" lands at all.
4. **Two ingredients need a formulator, not more desk research:** iodine and selenium at scale in an effervescent, and whether riboflavin's colour is manageable. Note that riboflavin already appears as an **undeclared colourant** in at least seven products, so the fluorescence objection is weaker than this project assumed.

## 6. The honest weaknesses

- **No demand evidence for combinations.** Nobody in the review data asked for them, just as nobody asked for more vitamins. This architecture is built from supply-side and claims-side gaps. **It is a hypothesis about what would be good, not evidence of what is wanted.**
- **Iodine may be absent for a reason nobody has told us.** Three products out of 71 is either a genuine gap or a signal. A formulator will know in one conversation; the desk cannot.
- **Declared panels understate formulations.** Several products list nutrients in the ingredients and omit them from the panel (A-087), so "nobody carries X" means "nobody declares X".
- **This is a sketch by the CSO, not the proposition work.** `proposition-architect` should produce two or three genuinely different architectures and `red-team` should attack them before anything goes to Gate 2.
