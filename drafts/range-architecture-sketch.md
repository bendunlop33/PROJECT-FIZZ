# Range Architecture — Sketch

**Author:** CSO · **Date:** 2026-09-22
**Status: a SKETCH for the user to react to, not a recommendation and not a decision pack.** `proposition-architect` has not run and Gate 2 has not been reached. Every number below is transcribed from `research/ingredient-benchmark.csv` (97 products) or the parsed GB NHC register; the *choices* are OPINION.

---

## 1. The strategic frame, corrected

The user's framing on 2026-09-22: **this is not about inventing something nobody has. It is about cementing a niche — functional, vitamin-led, flavoured cubes for your water bottle — with enough range that one product is not trying to do everything.**

That is a better frame than the one the research has been chasing, and the evidence supports it. Three findings converge:

1. **Waterdrop defined the cube and owns the flavour-led end of it.** 2.0–2.3 g, twelve flavours, and a vitamin load deliberately held at 15–50% NRV (§3 of `cube-category-map.md`).
2. **The functional end of the cube is unoccupied.** Phizz Daily Immune+ has the function — the complete authorised immune set and the complete iron system — but it is a tablet in a tube, pharmacy-coded, sold through Holland & Barrett, and does not market on the interaction at all. Its own site publishes no panel.
3. **Nobody has copied the cube form factor in four years** (`cube-category-map.md` §6). Whatever that means, it is not a crowded niche.

**So the niche is: the cube, done functionally.** Owning it is a brand and consistency problem, not an invention problem. The question stops being "what can we make that nobody has" and becomes "what would the definitive version of this look like".

## 2. What a range has to satisfy

Six constraints, all already evidenced. Any architecture that breaks one is wrong.

| # | Constraint | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **2.0–2.3 g per cube.** Bulky minerals at full NRV are impossible; µg and low-mg nutrients are nearly free | A-066 |
| 2 | **Taste degrades as dose climbs.** Observed band: light 4.7 → 25–50% NRV 4.1 → 375% 3.5 → 595%+ 2.8. **Design inside 50–100% NRV** | A-076 |
| 3 | **Flavour intensity per cube at 500 ml is the category's biggest unfixed complaint**, five years old, with a two-cube workaround that doubles the customer's cost | `review-mining.md` |
| 4 | **Every authorised combination claim needs only 15% NRV** — so combinations are free in mass and sit below the taste ceiling | A-009, §7 of `category-benchmarks.md` |
| 5 | **Benefit-led names may themselves be health claims.** Waterdrop's surviving names are composition statements | A-074 |
| 6 | **Under D2C-only, each SKU must not be a separate acquisition funnel.** One brand, one format, one occasion; the range is a choice at checkout | A-028 |

## 3. Three SKUs, each with a job the market demonstrably leaves open

Each does one thing, passes its own category's benchmark (`category-benchmarks.md`), and fills a hole the 97-product benchmark actually shows.

### SKU 1 — the everyday cube · *"Vitamins + Minerals"*

**The job:** the daily multivitamin you drink instead of swallow.

**The hole it fills, and it is the widest one in the dataset.** Of 71 panelled products, only **2** carry all five coverage markers — vitamin D, riboflavin, folate, iodine, selenium. **38 carry none of them.** And **iodine is the rarest nutrient in the entire UK market: 3 of 71**, of which one is a children's product and one is a swallowed tablet. **No adult everyday-vitamin drink in the UK declares iodine.** No Waterdrop product declares vitamin D, iodine or selenium at all.

**Spec direction:** the five markers, plus C and the B-complex, everything in the 50–100% NRV band. All five markers are µg-to-low-mg, so the mass cost is trivial — the reason nobody carries them is not that they cannot.

**Why it holds:** this is a coverage claim, not a dose claim. Waterdrop can raise a dose in an afternoon; adding iodine and selenium to a range it has never carried is a reformulation across twelve SKUs.

### SKU 2 — the hydration cube · *"Electrolytes + Vitamins"*

**The job:** actually rehydrate, which the category leader does not.

**The hole:** **16 of 41 hydration products declare no sodium at all.** Waterdrop Microlyte Berry declares 0.16 g salt — roughly **63 mg sodium** — against a category range of 116–1,034 mg, and declares no potassium despite potassium chloride being its second ingredient.

**Spec direction:** a real, declared sodium and potassium figure. Sodium and potassium salts are light and cheap; this is not a mass problem. Out-specifying the format's leader on the format's own benchmark is available for pennies.

### SKU 3 — the antioxidant cube · *"Antioxidant Complex"*

**The job:** the range's functional flagship, and the one genuinely unbuilt thing in the dataset.

**The hole:** the authorised *"contributes to the protection of cells from oxidative stress"* claim belongs to exactly seven nutrients — **copper, manganese, riboflavin, selenium, vitamin C, vitamin E, zinc**. **No UK drink completes the set.** The best are three at 6/7, each missing a different one: High5 (copper), Phizz 3-in-1 (selenium), Phizz Immune+ (manganese). Only Centrum, a swallowed tablet, completes it.

**Spec direction:** all seven. Every one is µg-to-low-mg, so the complete set fits a 2.0 g cube comfortably, and every one needs only 15% NRV — well below the taste ceiling.

**Why this is the flagship:** it is the only place where the brand can say *seven nutrients, all seven of the ones the law recognises, nobody else has done it* — and have that be literally true and legally sayable.

## 3A. The candidate slot map — scarcity × claim set

**CSO CORRECTION FIRST.** This project has twice said "focus" has only one authorised sentence (pantothenic acid → normal mental performance). **That was wrong.** The word *focus* is not claimable, but the territory around it is far better supported than recorded:

- **Cognitive function — 3 nutrients: iodine, iron, zinc.** A complete, small, authorised set.
- **Psychological function — 8: biotin, folate, magnesium, niacin, thiamine, B12, B6, vitamin C.**
- **Nervous system — 11:** the above plus copper, iodine, potassium, riboflavin.
- **Mental performance — 1: pantothenic acid.**

**And the striking part: iodine and iron are the two scarcest nutrients in the entire UK market (3/71 and 5/71) — and they are two of the three nutrients carrying the cognitive-function claim.** A cognitive SKU is therefore both the best-claimed and the least-contested territory available, with no caffeine and no botanical.

### The scarcity map — where the whitespace physically is

From `ingredient-benchmark.csv`, 71 panelled UK products. **Scarcity is the opportunity: a nutrient almost nobody declares is a nutrient nobody can answer quickly.**

| Nutrient | Declared by | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| **Iodine** | **3/71 (4%)** | cognitive function · nervous system · thyroid · skin · **no adult everyday-vitamin drink in the UK declares it** |
| **Iron** | **5/71 (7%)** | 8 claims of its own — cognitive, immune, tiredness, red blood cells, oxygen transport, cell division |
| **Copper** | **6/71 (9%)** | oxidative stress · nervous system · iron transport · immune |
| **Manganese** | **6/71 (9%)** | oxidative stress · connective tissue · bones |
| **Vitamin A** | **7/71 (10%)** | iron metabolism · immune · skin · vision |
| **Selenium** | **9/71 (13%)** | oxidative stress · immune · thyroid · hair · nails |
| **Vitamin E** | **9/71 (13%)** | oxidative stress |
| **Vitamin D** | **13/71 (18%)** | immune · bones · muscle · calcium absorption |
| Vitamin K | 4/71 (6%) | bones · blood clotting |

### Five candidate slots, each a complete authorised set on scarce nutrients

| Slot | The complete set it can own | Scarcity anchor | Occupied? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Everyday / Multi** | the five coverage markers: D, B2, folate, **iodine**, selenium | iodine 3/71 · D 13/71 | **2 of 71 products carry all five; 38 carry none** |
| **Mind / Cognitive** | cognitive function complete: **iodine + iron + zinc** (all 3), plus 8 psychological and 11 nervous-system nutrients | **iodine + iron — the two scarcest** | No UK drink carries all three at ≥15% NRV |
| **Restore / Iron** | the iron system complete: **iron + C + copper + B2 + A** (all 5), plus iron's own 8 claims and the 4-nutrient red-blood-cell set | iron 5/71 · copper 6/71 · A 7/71 | **Phizz Daily Immune+ completes it** — but sells it as immunity |
| **Defence / Antioxidant** | oxidative stress complete: copper, **manganese**, B2, selenium, C, E, zinc (all 7) | manganese 6/71 · copper 6/71 | **No UK drink completes it.** Best are three at 6/7 |
| **Hydrate** | no authorised hydration claim exists for an enhancer — competes on declared sodium and potassium instead | — | 16 of 41 hydration products declare no sodium |

**Note what the scarcity column shows: iodine, iron, copper, manganese and vitamin A between them anchor four of the five slots, and not one of them is carried by Waterdrop in any product.** That is the concrete shape of the whitespace.

### On a women's variant

The user's instinct is well supported by the claim data — **iron carries eight authorised claims, more than almost any nutrient**, and around it sit B6 → *regulation of hormonal activity*, zinc → *fertility and reproduction*, folate → *maternal tissue growth during pregnancy*, and the Article 14 folic acid neural-tube claim at 400 µg with its mandatory accompanying statement.

**But two logged findings argue against gendering it.** A-036 found that **gender does not price** — Boots charges the same for men's and women's — and the user's own 2026-09-22 decision set variants as **lifestyle-based rather than gender-based**.

**The reconciliation: build the iron product, do not gender it.** Iron deficiency is not exclusively female, iron is the second-scarcest nutrient in the market, and the iron system is a complete authorised combination. A *Restore* or *Iron+* SKU serves the same customer need, carries the same claims, avoids the A-036 pricing finding and respects the logged decision. Gender-specific formulation can sit underneath it later if the evidence supports it.

**One hard constraint on any iron SKU: taste.** Iron is metallic in an effervescent and this range's whole premise is tasting good. The precedent exists — **Berocca Mind ships 12 mg of iron in an effervescent tablet today** — so it is solvable, but it is the single biggest formulation risk in the range and should be prototyped before the SKU is committed.

## 3B. The "two a day" spec — and why it resolves almost everything

User spec, 2026-09-22: **medium-to-high efficacy, designed so you could take two in a day, tasty, with a clear product architecture.**

Two a day is the most consequential constraint given so far, because it sets the dosing arithmetic — and it happens to resolve three problems this project had been treating as separate.

### 3B.1 The category leader already assumes two a day. It just doesn't dose for it.

**FACT.** Waterdrop's "30 Day Hydration Set" is **60 servings** (https://en.waterdrop.com/products/microdrink-blackcurrant, read 2026-09-22). Its own pack maths assumes **two cubes per day**.

**FACT.** The largest coherent product complaint in the review data is that **one cube does not adequately flavour 500 ml**, with an independently-reported workaround of using two — *"you need two cubes to make a good flavour in a pint so not as cheap as would like"* (`research/review-mining.md`).

**So the customer is already taking two, the brand's own pack is built for two, and the formulation is dosed for one.** That is the gap stated as plainly as it can be: **Waterdrop assumes two and doses for one. Dose for two.**

### 3B.2 The dosing rule this produces is clean

**Set each cube at 50% NRV. Two cubes then deliver exactly 100% NRV of the day's needs.**

| Nutrient | Per cube (50% NRV) | Two cubes | Approx. mass per cube |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vitamin D | 2.5 µg | **100% NRV** | ~12 mg as CWS powder |
| Iodine | 75 µg | **100% NRV** | ~0.1 mg as potassium iodide |
| Selenium | 27.5 µg | **100% NRV** | ~0.07 mg as sodium selenite |
| Folate | 100 µg | **100% NRV** | ~0.1 mg |
| Vitamin C | 40 mg | **100% NRV** | 40 mg |
| Zinc | 5 mg | **100% NRV** | ~25 mg as bisglycinate |

**About 80 mg of actives per cube.** Against a 2.0 g cube of which perhaps 0.5–1.0 g is available after the acid–carbonate couple, that leaves the overwhelming majority of the free mass for **flavour** — which is the differentiator. **Efficacy and taste stop competing.**

**It also gives the proposition a sentence that is true and simple: one cube is half your day, two is all of it.** Waterdrop's equivalent is 25–50% NRV on three to nine nutrients at one cube. Berocca's is 300–970% on a B-complex.

**Vitamin D is worth an exception.** Its NRV is only 5 µg, while NHS advice is 10 µg daily through the winter. Dosing D at 5 µg per cube (100% NRV each) gives **10 µg at two a day — the NHS winter figure** — and remains far below any upper level. **ESTIMATE/OPINION; confirm with the safety check below.**

### 3B.3 The safety constraint, stated honestly

**A product designed for two a day must be safe at two a day, and that is a different test from a single serving.** The binding question is not whether one cube sits under the NRV but whether **two cubes sit under the upper level** — and the nutrients with narrow headroom between NRV and upper level are the ones that decide the formulation: **iron, zinc, selenium, iodine, vitamin A, vitamin B6, copper and manganese.** Vitamin C and most B vitamins have wide headroom and are not the constraint.

**I could not retrieve the EVM *Safe Upper Levels for Vitamins and Minerals* (2003) tables this session** — cot.food.gov.uk returned 404 on both paths and the EU mirror rate-limited. **So no upper-level figure is asserted anywhere in this document.** This is a named, cheap, specific task for `claims-regulatory`: pull the EVM SULs and Guidance Levels and set the per-cube ceiling for each nutrient at half the supplemental upper level. **Do it before any dose is fixed.**

**One design consequence is already clear, though: if the two cubes carry *different* nutrients, nothing doubles.** The upper-level problem only bites when someone takes two of the same cube — so the Core must be safe at 2×, and the second cube should carry a largely different nutrient set. That is not just a safety accommodation; it is the architecture.

### 3B.4 The architecture the spec implies — Core + Choice

**Cube one: the Core.** The efficacy base, same every day — vitamin D, iodine, selenium, folate, vitamin C, zinc at 50% NRV each. This is the subscription anchor and the single acquisition funnel.

**Cube two: your choice.** A different nutrient set, chosen by the customer, taken with the second bottle:

| Second cube | Carries | Nutrient overlap with Core |
|---|---|---|
| **Restore** | iron + copper + vitamin A (+ the C in the Core completes the absorption pair) | minimal |
| **Defence** | vitamin E + manganese + copper (+ Core's C, zinc, selenium complete the antioxidant set) | complementary, not duplicative |
| **Hydrate** | sodium, potassium, magnesium, chloride | **none** |
| **Mind** | iron + the B-complex (+ Core's iodine and zinc complete the cognitive set) | minimal |

**Why this shape is right, and it satisfies every constraint the project has accumulated:**

- **One product everyone buys.** The Core is the funnel — a single acquisition story, which is the only shape A-028 permits under D2C-only. The range lives in the second cube, which is sold to an existing customer, not acquired separately.
- **It answers "one product can't do everything" without multiplying funnels.** The user's exact objection, resolved structurally.
- **The combinations complete across the pair, not within one cube.** Core + Restore is the iron system. Core + Defence is the antioxidant set. That is more interesting than either cube alone and it is a genuine reason to buy two.
- **Nothing doubles**, so the upper-level headroom is spent once.
- **Two cubes = two bottles = a litre of water**, which supports the drink-more-water behaviour the format exists for.
- **Sixty cubes a month is a real subscription** — and it matches Waterdrop's own pack cadence, so the usage assumption is not ours alone.
- **Flavour variety across the pair** answers the flavour-fatigue theme in the review data.

**The honest caveats, unchanged:** the taste test has not happened; iron's metallic note in an effervescent is unsolved and constrains the Restore cube specifically; magnesium cannot be meaningfully dosed in any cube, so Hydrate is a source of it and not a solution; and **nobody in the review data asked for any of this.**

## 4. Why three, and why these three

- **Three distinct jobs, no overlap.** Everyday coverage · hydration · antioxidant. A customer can articulate which one they want without help, which is the test the Vitamin Well range fails (Reload and Recover are formulation-identical).
- **They share one funnel.** Same brand, same cube, same occasion — a cube in your water bottle. The range is a choice at checkout, not three products to market. That is the only shape A-028 permits.
- **Each passes its own benchmark**, so none is a product wearing a badge it has not earned — the failure mode found in 16 of 41 hydration products, and in Microlyte specifically.
- **It respects the logged 3-product cap** without pretending the cap was arbitrary.

**Deliberately excluded, with reasons:** *energy* (needs caffeine to mean anything, and caffeine is a decided no — and carries no authorised claim regardless, A-052); *focus* (one authorised sentence, no felt mechanism, A-052); *sleep, calm, stress* (no authorised GB claim exists at all — the trap in `opportunity-areas.md`); *immune* (Phizz Daily Immune+ already holds the complete set, so it is the one territory that is genuinely taken).

## 5. What must be decided before this is worth costing

1. **Naming (A-074).** "Daily", "Restore", "Defend" are benefit names and may be implied health claims. Waterdrop's surviving names are all composition statements — *Hydration + Vitamins*, *+ Electrolytes*, *+ Energy* — which is a strong hint. **Default to composition naming until the food-law opinion lands.**
2. **Flavour intensity.** The whole proposition rests on tasting good, and the category's five-year-old unfixed complaint is that one cube does not flavour 500 ml. **Fixing that is the product, not a detail.**
3. **The taste test.** Nothing in this project measures taste. Six products, thirty people, one afternoon — it would settle both the flavour-intensity target and whether "designed to work together" lands at all.
4. **Two ingredients need a formulator, not more desk research:** iodine and selenium at scale in an effervescent, and whether riboflavin's colour is manageable. Note that riboflavin already appears as an **undeclared colourant** in at least seven products, so the fluorescence objection is weaker than this project assumed.

---

## 6. The honest weaknesses

- **No demand evidence for combinations.** Nobody in the review data asked for them, just as nobody asked for more vitamins. This architecture is built from supply-side and claims-side gaps. **It is a hypothesis about what would be good, not evidence of what is wanted.**
- **Iodine may be absent for a reason nobody has told us.** Three products out of 71 is either a genuine gap or a signal. A formulator will know in one conversation; the desk cannot.
- **Declared panels understate formulations.** Several products list nutrients in the ingredients and omit them from the panel (A-087), so "nobody carries X" means "nobody declares X".
- **This is a sketch by the CSO, not the proposition work.** `proposition-architect` should produce two or three genuinely different architectures and `red-team` should attack them before anything goes to Gate 2.
