# Market Mapping Brief — Positioning First

**Commissioned by:** CSO
**Date:** 2026-09-22
**Primary owner:** `market-mapper`. §5 (brand codes) may be split with `customer-insight`; §7 is a `red-team` hand-off.
**Status of this document:** a scope of work, not a finding. Nothing in here is evidence.

> ### Hard constraint — user direction, 2026-09-22
> **No model, and no analysis that depends on a chain of estimates.**
>
> No spreadsheet. No market sizing, TAM or SAM. No forecasts, no growth rates, no share estimates, no quantified opportunity. The priority is understanding the market as it actually is.
>
> The test for every deliverable in this brief: **could someone verify it by looking?** A brand's own page, a search results page, an advertisement, a pack, a withdrawn product. That is the evidence this work runs on. Anything you arrive at by assuming your way to a number does not belong here — and a number is the most confident-looking thing on a page, which is exactly why it is the most dangerous when it is invented.
>
> Where judgement is unavoidable, make it one labelled OPINION sentence, not a calculation.

---

## 0. Why this brief exists

We already have a market map. It is price-led: `research/competitor-map.md` labels the price ladder and the price-to-potency comparison as "the priority section", and `research/vitamin-category-map.md` is built on the same spine. That work was correct for the question being asked at the time — *can V-Drop win a price-to-potency argument?* — and it answered it clearly: **no**, at £0.833/serving V-Drop would be the dearest effervescent tablet in a 77-row dataset.

Two user decisions on 2026-09-22 then changed the question underneath it:

1. **The differentiator is taste and ease of use — not potency, not claims, not ingredients.** That decision explicitly retires the price-to-potency argument. Most of our sharpest analysis is aimed at a fight we have chosen not to have.
2. **Positioning is "clinical but tasty and cool", explicitly not sport.** That is a positioning statement, and we have never mapped the market as a positioning problem. We mapped it as a pricing problem and inferred positioning from the leftovers.

So this brief commissions the work that should have come first: **the category mapping a strategy consultant runs before a brand develops the idea**, not after. It is a distinct piece of work from the existing map, not a revision of it.

**Price is deliberately out of scope.** See §2 for the one narrow exception.

---

## 1. The question this work must answer

> **In the buyer's head, what category is this product in, who is the brand they already think of for that job, and is there a position we can take that is empty, believable from a new brand, and hard to copy?**

Four sub-questions, in priority order. If the work runs out of time, it answers them in this order and stops honestly.

| # | Question | Why it is load-bearing |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | **Compared to what?** What does the buyer put V-Drop next to when deciding — and, under D2C-only, where does that comparison physically happen? | Under the D2C-only decision the comparison is not on a shelf. It happens in search results and in a paid-social feed. We have never established what that adjacency looks like, so we do not actually know who we compete with. |
| Q2 | **Instead of what?** Which existing purchase or habit does the money come out of? | "Share of throat" is a fundraising phrase until you name the thing it displaces. Every territory we claim needs a substitution hypothesis. |
| Q3 | **Is "clinical but tasty and cool" empty, or is it a graveyard?** | An unoccupied position is only an opportunity if nobody has tried it. If brands have tried and withdrawn, the space is empty for a reason. A-050 already logs this doubt in a different context. |
| Q4 | **What stops Bayer, Vitabiotics, Holland & Barrett or a supermarket own-label taking it within 12 months?** | A-009 establishes that the authorised claims behind this proposition are available to a £0.06/serving Tesco effervescent. Claims cannot be the moat. Something else has to be, or the position is rented. |

## 2. Scope

**In scope:** category definition and frames of reference; category entry points; competitive adjacency as the customer encounters it; positioning axes derived from customer language; brand codes, verbal and visual; whitespace stress-testing; category-creation precedents; defensibility.

**Out of scope — do not reopen:** unit economics, margin, COGS, CAC, subscription LTV, pack architecture, postage, the price ladder, price-per-serving and price-per-gram-of-active comparisons. All of that is `pricing-economist`'s and is either done or deliberately parked. Do not re-derive it, do not re-argue it, and do not let a positioning finding be expressed as a pricing recommendation.

**Also out of scope, per the constraint above:** market sizing, TAM and SAM, growth forecasts, category share estimates, and any spreadsheet whatsoever. `model/unit-economics.xlsx` stays where it is — not opened, not extended, not cited. It is parked, not wrong; `drafts/pricing-recommendation.md` likewise.

**This brief overrides the `market-mapper` agent's standing instructions where the two conflict.** That agent's default scope makes the price ladder and `research/competitors.csv` its priority sections. For this piece of work neither is required, and neither should be produced or updated. Its scoring rubrics (functional intensity, portability) stay available if a positioning map needs them.

**The one exception.** Price may be treated as a **positioning signal** — a quality cue, a permission-to-believe, a shelf-position statement — where it is doing semiotic work rather than arithmetic. "LYMA's £199 price point is itself the claim" is in scope. "V-Drop's contribution margin at £0.833 is 9.0%" is not. If a section starts producing decimals, it has drifted.

**Geography:** Great Britain. Non-GB brands enter only where a GB buyer would realistically encounter them in search or social.

## 3. The three hypotheses this work must test

State a verdict on each. These are the CSO's hypotheses, not settled views — disconfirming any of them is a good outcome and more useful than confirming it.

**H1 — The frame of reference is the morning multivitamin, not the effervescent tablet.**
The user has set Wild Nutrition as the positioning reference and explicitly rejected Berocca. If true, our competitive set is capsules and powders and our format is the differentiator. If false — if buyers file us next to Berocca and Phizz whatever we intend — then our price and our story are both being read against the wrong ladder and the proposition has a structural problem. *This is the single most consequential question in the brief.*

**H2 — "Clinical" and "cool" are combinable in this category, and nobody has combined them.**
The premise behind the positioning decision. It rests on a reading of five brands (LYMA, Berocca, Waterdrop, Spacegoods, DIRTEA) recorded in the decisions log. Five is not a basis. Test it against a proper brand-codes audit (§5) and say whether the combination is unoccupied, occupied, or incoherent.

**H3 — In GB, the effervescent tablet format carries an inherited meaning of *remedy*, not *ritual*.** — NEW, and unlogged.
This came out of the attached HL deck. Its German section makes a pure positioning observation: *"German consumers mainly associate electrolytes with sports performance, recovery or after exertion. Electrolyte supplementation for wellness / health / lifestyle is growing but 'less mainstream'."* That is a statement about inherited category meaning, and it is exactly the kind of thing that kills a proposition quietly.

The GB equivalent question: what does a British consumer think a fizzy tablet in water *is for*? The available cultural references are Berocca (hangover), Beechams and Lemsip (illness), effervescent vitamin C (a cold coming on), Dioralyte (gastroenteritis), Alka-Seltzer (indigestion). Every one of them is **remedial, episodic and slightly medicinal** — you take it because something is wrong. V-Drop's entire concept is the opposite: a daily, well-person, pleasurable morning ritual.

If that inherited meaning is strong, "clinical but tasty and cool" is not a fresh position — it is a fight against what the format already means, and the "cool" half is doing far more work than anyone has budgeted for. Note this also sharpens A-039 (the natural/effervescent conflict) from an ingredient-list problem into a semiotic one.

**Log H3 as A-065 on first evidence either way.** Do not leave it as a brief-only observation.

## 4. Method

Six workstreams. **W1–W3 are the core and must be completed. W4–W6 are supporting** and may be cut back if the core takes longer than expected — say so rather than thinning all six.

### W1 — Share of throat, done honestly (Q1, Q2)

Build the adjacency map: V-Drop at the centre, surrounded by the consumption territories it could take share from, each populated with real GB brands.

Candidate territories, to be confirmed and amended — this list is a starting point, not an answer:
morning multivitamin (capsules, gummies) · effervescent vitamins and tonics · greens and AG1-class morning powders · functional mushroom · hydration and electrolyte · the morning coffee ritual · breakfast juice and smoothies · energy drinks · plain water · pharmacy wellness.

**The technique is borrowed from the attached HOLY slide, and so is the warning.** HOLY's "share of throat" chart is a good thinking structure and a bad analysis. It arranges six adjacent categories around the brand, fills each with peer logos, and lets proximity imply addressability — without a single line of evidence that HOLY takes a pound from any of them. It is a fundraising artefact: its job is to make a TAM look large. Steal the structure; do not inherit the logic.

So, for every territory, the map must carry:
- the GB brands actually in it, with the one V-Drop would be measured against;
- a **substitution hypothesis** in one sentence — *what specific purchase stops, or what habit changes, when someone adopts V-Drop?*;
- the evidence for that hypothesis, and its strength;
- a **displacement verdict: real / partial / rhetorical.** A territory that scores "rhetorical" stays on the diagram, greyed, with its verdict printed next to it. It does not get quietly dropped, and it does not get counted.

**Deliverable:** `research/share-of-throat.md` plus a diagram in `research/charts/`.

**Do not size the territories.** Name who is in each one and what we would be displacing. A number beside a territory is a guess wearing a suit, and it is the specific thing the user has ruled out — it is also, precisely, what makes the HOLY slide unusable as analysis.

### W2 — Category entry points (Q1)

Positioning is a seller's word. Buyers have **entry points**: the situations that trigger a purchase. Map them.

- Enumerate the situations that send a GB adult looking for something like this. Draw the raw material from the existing customer language bank (`research/customer-segments.md` §7) rather than inventing them, and extend it. Expect things like *I'm shattered every morning* · *I keep forgetting to take my vitamins* · *I'm trying to cut down on coffee* · *I don't drink enough water* · *I want one thing instead of six bottles* · *I want to feel like I'm looking after myself*.
- For each entry point, name the **first-thought GB brand** and the evidence for it. That brand is the real competitor for that occasion, whatever the format.
- Mark which entry points the O3 07:00–09:00 habit slot actually owns. The morning-drink decision bets the whole proposition on O3, and `customer-segments.md` §2 supports it as the best retention occasion — but retention and *acquisition* are different, and the entry point that gets someone to buy the first pack may not be the one that keeps them.
- Flag any entry point where the honest first-thought answer is "coffee" or "nothing".

**Deliverable:** an entry-point table in `research/positioning-map.md`, ranked by how contestable each is for a new brand.

### W3 — The D2C frame of reference (Q1) — *the one most likely to change our mind*

The D2C-only decision means there is no shelf. The frame of reference is set by the search results page and the social feed. We have never looked at either. Until we do, our competitive set is a hypothesis.

- **Search landscape.** For the terms a buyer would plausibly use — *morning vitamin drink*, *effervescent multivitamin*, *daily vitamin drink UK*, *vitamin drink instead of coffee*, *best multivitamin UK*, and whatever the language bank suggests — record what actually returns: organic results, who is paying for the term, which retailers dominate, and whether the SERP is informational or transactional. Read the pages; do not work from search snippets (see §6).
- **Amazon adjacency.** For the same terms, what does Amazon UK show, and what sits in "customers also bought"? That block is a machine-generated statement of the real competitive set and it does not flatter anyone.
- **Paid social adjacency.** Use the Meta Ad Library for the GB market: who is running vitamin and functional-drink creative, what the creative says, which entry point it leads with, and what it looks like. Note the **format of the argument** (founder piece-to-camera, clinical authority, before-and-after, taste), not just the brand.
- **Verdict on H1.** From this evidence, state plainly: does the market file a premium effervescent morning multivitamin next to Wild Nutrition, or next to Berocca? Do not soften this. If it is Berocca, say so — the user has explicitly rejected Berocca as a reference, and a decision that collides with evidence must be flagged under house rule 3, not worked around.

**Deliverable:** `research/frame-of-reference.md`, with screenshots or verbatim result lists, dated.

### W4 — Positioning axes, derived rather than assumed

Our existing maps use price × functional intensity and design cue × portability. All four are **seller's axes** — they are how a category analyst sees the market, not how a buyer does. Price is also now out of scope.

- Derive 8–10 candidate axes from customer language (W2's raw material and `customer-segments.md` §7).
- Test each against two filters: **does it discriminate** between real brands, and **does it matter** to buyers — is there evidence they talk about it unprompted?
- Keep the three that pass both. Discard the rest visibly, with the reason.
- Plot 15–20 GB brands on the surviving axes and mark where "clinical but tasty and cool" lands.

Candidate axes worth testing, offered as prompts rather than a shortlist: clinical ↔ natural · effortful ↔ effortless · treat ↔ regimen · subtle ↔ perceptible effect · expert authority ↔ peer recommendation · single-purpose ↔ all-in-one · medicinal ↔ delicious · well-person ↔ fixing-a-problem (this last one carries H3).

**Deliverable:** maps in `research/positioning-map.md` and `research/charts/`.

### W5 — Brand codes audit (H2)

For 12–15 GB-available brands spanning clinical, premium-natural, design-led and functional positions — LYMA, Wild Nutrition, Berocca, Waterdrop, Feel, Heights, Form, Innermost, Ancient + Brave, Bioglan, Vitabiotics, Spacegoods, DIRTEA, AG1, plus any the earlier workstreams surface — record:

colour palette · typography · naming convention · tone of voice · hero imagery (product, person, ingredient, science) · pack architecture · founder visibility · the **one word** the brand is trying to own · which of *clinical* / *tasty* / *cool* it credibly holds, and the specific cues that earn it.

The output is not a moodboard. It is an answer to one question: **what are the codes of "clinical" in this category, what are the codes of "cool", and does anything happen when you combine them other than incoherence?** Name any brand that has genuinely done both, and if none has, say whether that is whitespace or a warning.

**Deliverable:** `research/brand-codes.md`.

### W6 — Whitespace stress test and precedent (Q3, Q4)

Two halves, both sceptical.

**Graveyard check.** For each apparent gap the earlier workstreams surface, ask who has tried it. Search for withdrawn products, discontinued lines, brands that pivoted away, and UK launches that did not survive 24 months. An empty space with wreckage in it is not whitespace. A-050 already records "whitespace confirmed, demand doubted" in another context — treat that as the default posture, not the exception.

**Category-creation precedent.** Five brands that created or re-framed a category rather than competing inside one. The attached HL deck nominates ON and yfood explicitly as having "shaped a new category"; AG1, Liquid I.V., Waterdrop, HOLY and Humantra itself are the on-category candidates. For each, isolate **the positioning move**, not the growth story: did they name a new category or re-position inside an old one? What did the first 18 months of communication actually say? What did they refuse to be?

**Defensibility.** Close on Q4. For the position we land on, name what stops a large incumbent occupying it within 12 months. Claims cannot be the answer — A-009 settles that. Be willing to conclude that nothing does.

**Deliverable:** `research/whitespace-and-precedent.md`.

## 5. How to use the attached material

Three inputs came with this brief. They are useful, and none of them is evidence about our market.

**HOLY "Share of Throat" slide.** A structural template for W1 and a worked example of the failure mode W1 must avoid. Discussed in full at W1.

**Houlihan Lokey, *Humantra — International Expansion Perspectives*, September 2025.** A sell-side banking deck prepared for a company considering US and German expansion. Its value to us is almost entirely in **how the category talks about itself**:
- the trend taxonomy — low sugar, personalised multi-benefit, sustainability, sports and performance — and the format diversification frame (tablets and powders, drops, gummies; "Hydration Plus" as +immunity / +energy / +protein; "Nature's Hydration");
- the observation that German participants are mostly **extending into hydration from an adjacent core category** rather than starting there, which is a useful lens on who might enter our space from outside it;
- the German consumer-association insight that generates H3;
- the ON and yfood category-creation cases for W6.

Use it for none of the following: market sizes, CAGRs, growth rates, or share figures. It is a pitch document, its headline numbers are sourced to Grand View Research and Precedence Research, and its own page carries the note *"Competitors from general google research"*. Which brings us to:

**A standing instruction on sell-side market sizing.** Grand View, Precedence, Cognitive Market Research and their peers sell syndicated reports; their public figures are marketing for those reports, their methodologies are not disclosed, and their category definitions are elastic. Numbers of that provenance may appear in our work **only** as illustrative context, labelled ESTIMATE, attributed to the specific report, and never as a load-bearing input to a recommendation. They must not enter `notes/assumptions.md` as FACT. This is the same discipline house rule 1 already applies to unlinked references, extended to a source type that looks more authoritative than it is. The HOLY slide's "$1.2tr → $2.0tr at 7.4% CAGR" is the type specimen.

**`Hydration Perspectives 2025`** is a subset of the Humantra deck's first section — same slides, same sources. Treat it as one input, not two.

## 6. Evidence standards

House rules 1, 6, 7 and 9 in `CLAUDE.md` apply in full — sourced URLs with check dates, FACT/ESTIMATE/OPINION labelling on every statement, British English, and summarising in your own words. On top of those, four things specific to this brief:

1. **Read pages directly.** As of 2026-09-22 the egress restrictions that forced earlier sessions onto search-engine summaries have largely lifted: gov.uk, legislation.gov.uk, wildnutrition.com, ankhway.com and appliednutrition.uk were all read directly this session. **Re-test before assuming any domain is blocked** — several "blocked" notes in the register are now out of date. Known still-refusing, and refusing at their own edge rather than ours: `royalmail.com`, `boots.com`, `mailcoms.co.uk`. Record *how* a page failed, not just that it did.
2. **Positioning claims need a primary artefact.** A statement about how a brand positions itself is evidenced by the brand's own page, pack or advertising — not by a listicle, a review site or a competitor's deck. Screenshot or quote briefly, and date it.
3. **Separate observation from inference, visibly.** "Waterdrop's palette is pastel and its hero image is the object, not a person" is an observation. "Waterdrop is therefore selling design over function" is an inference. The existing research is generally good at this; keep it up.
4. **Prefer no number to a soft one.** If a question can be answered by description, describe it. If it can only be answered by estimating, the honest output is the named gap, not the estimate. Nothing from a syndicated market-sizing house enters the register as FACT, per §5.

## 8. What good looks like

- It answers Q1 with evidence from what a buyer actually sees, not from a category taxonomy we invented.
- It gives a **straight verdict on H1**, and does not hedge if the verdict collides with the Wild Nutrition / not-Berocca decision. A collision is a finding; flagging it is house rule 3.
- Every territory in W1 carries a substitution hypothesis, and at least one is honestly marked *rhetorical*.
- The positioning axes are ones a customer would recognise, and the discarded candidates are visible.
- H3 gets a real answer. If the effervescent format reads as *remedy* in GB, that is the most important thing this work can tell us, and it should lead the summary.
- It is willing to conclude that the position is not defensible. Q4 has no required answer.

**Failure modes, named so they can be avoided:**
- Rebuilding the price ladder under a new heading.
- A share-of-throat diagram with logos and no substitution evidence — the HOLY failure.
- Treating syndicated market sizes as facts because they carry a research house's name.
- Confirming H1 and H2 because the user has already decided them. They are decisions about intent; this brief tests whether the market agrees, and the user has asked for honest challenge throughout this project.
- Six workstreams at half depth. Three finished beats six started.
- Producing a number where an observation was asked for — a sized territory, a modelled opportunity, a forecast. The brief has no numeric deliverables by design.
