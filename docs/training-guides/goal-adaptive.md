**Summary**
1. Ben's confirmed **main focus** picks a profile: Strength, Muscle, General fitness, Maintain, or Endurance (where lifting supports a sport). The profile sets reps, effort, weekly sets, rest, session order and deload style. "Also matters" goals add minimum floors, and a named event adds a timeline. Grades: Strong for "heavy loads build strength" and "weekly sets drive size". Moderate for order, frequency, maintenance and tapers. Practice for the structure and the exact bands.
2. **How training is run** then adjusts the profile: days a week, session length, other training, equipment, experience, and whether Ben is following a named programme. Frequency and time set the volume (revised rule 1). Equipment sets the progression lever (revised rule 7).
3. **Rules 11–13 apply only when endurance-type sessions are planned, imported or reported.** That covers runs, rides, hard classes and sport. With none, the rules stay silent.
4. **Goals and phases change only after Ben confirms.** The coach may ask when it sees one of four clear signals. It then offers a goal proposal and a separate plan proposal, each with before/after, Apply and Undo. The quality Ben is moving away from keeps a maintenance dose.
5. Three refinements of approved rules need Ben's OK: rule 13 (1 heavy session a week in an endurance competition period; Weak), rule 3 (RPE 6–8 for general fitness and new lifters; Practice), and how the ADR 0006 "too easy" offer behaves during a taper or under Maintain.

### Conflicts and decisions for Ben

1. **Rule 13 (approved) vs GA6.** Rule 13 says to keep ≥ 2 heavy strength exposures a week through endurance phases. Two small trials (n = 6–7 per group) found that **1 heavy session a week** kept strength through a 12–13-week competition period: one in cyclists ([Rønnestad 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-010-1622-4)) and one in soccer players ([Rønnestad 2011](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/21873897)). The review by the same lead author says this "seems to maintain" gains. The proposed refinement: ≥ 2 a week by default; with an Endurance main focus in a race or competition period, 1 heavy session a week is the minimum. *This changes an approved rule, so Ben needs to approve it. Grade: Weak.*
2. **Rule 3 (approved) vs GA4/GA13.** Rule 3 defaults compounds to RPE 7–9. For General fitness and new lifters, the proposal is RPE 6–8.
   - Support: WHO asks only for "moderate or greater intensity", and ACSM 2026 says training to failure isn't needed for the average adult.
   - *Correction:* this earlier leaned on "novices misjudge effort by 4–5 reps". That comes from one study (Steele 2017). A later meta-analysis (Halperin 2022) found training status didn't affect accuracy, and lifters generally underestimated by about 1 rep. Whatever the band, the coach should lean on reps achieved.
   - The ADR 0006 "too easy" trigger is unaffected, because it fires only when the plan was ≥ 8.
   - *Refines an approved rule; Ben to approve. Grade: Practice.*
3. **Onboarding has no "General fitness" or "Endurance" main focus.** Main focus chips are currently Strength / Muscle / Maintain. GA1 needs General fitness and Endurance (lifting supports it) as main-focus options. It also needs an "Other training" field (type, usual days, which are key) and an event field (date, type, priority). The CHANGELOG already says other training will be collected. *Design change; designer and Ben.*
4. **Scope: the coach schedules lifting around endurance; it doesn't write endurance plans.** Brief build step 6 brings "permitted endurance imports", not endurance coaching. This guide assumes runs and rides come from Ben or another app. *Ben to confirm.*
5. **Does an event override the main focus in its final weeks?** The proposal: only if Ben marks it as his priority when he adds it, and only through phase proposals he applies. Otherwise the main focus stays and the coach shows trade-offs (brief: "Explain trade-offs when protecting a key endurance workout conflicts with lifting goals").
6. **What happens with no goal set?** The proposal: use the General fitness profile, labelled "No goal set — using general defaults", and never save it as Ben's goal. Onboarding allows skipping goals (onboarding.md: "Skipping all three saves nothing"). *Ben to confirm.*
7. **"Too easy" offers during a taper and under Maintain.** The proposal: off in taper weeks, and optional wording under Maintain. This only removes offers and never adds changes, but it alters how an approved ADR 0006 trigger behaves. *Needs Ben's approval and a note in ADR 0006.*
8. **Goal-change questions (GA16) are a new coach-initiated behaviour.** ADR 0006 currently allows only the in-session triggers, and README conflict 5 raises the same issue for volume reviews. The proposal: allow only GA16's four signals, in chat or summaries Ben opens. *Ben to decide; ADR note if approved.*
9. **Who counts as a "new lifter"?** V3's algorithm uses less than about 6 months, matching ACSM 2009's "intermediate ≈ 6 months of consistent training". GA13 and the onboarding chips use less than 1 year. The proposal: use the onboarding chip, and let ≥ 4 weeks of logs override the start caps (V3). *Ben or PM to pick one definition.*
10. **Wording of approved rule 13.** Its "applies when" still reads "Endurance/HYROX blocks". To match Ben's "stop with HYROX" feedback and GA15, it should read "when the endurance switch (GA15) is on". *Resolved 9 Oct in playbook v1 (now: the switch is on and endurance is one of Ben's goals).*

**Fact**
- **ACSM 2026 position stand** ("Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance: An Overview of Reviews").
  - An umbrella review of 137 systematic reviews. Authors are listed with Phillips first; the deck doesn't name a chair. The summary deck is free; the full text is paywalled.
  - Table 6 lists what *enhances* each outcome compared with standard training:
    - Strength: "≥2 sessions/week", "≥80% 1RM (dose-response)", full range of motion, "2-3 sets/session", and exercise order at the "Beginning of training session".
    - Hypertrophy: "≥10 sets/week (dose-response)".
    - Power: 30–70% 1RM, with low to moderate volume (reps × sets < 24).
    - Muscular endurance: training improves it compared with no exercise, but which prescription enhances it is "ND" (insufficient data to form a conclusion).
    - Running performance: ND for training vs no training; velocity-based training enhanced it.
  - The discussion slides say "Individualization is More Important Than Rigid Rules". They add that training to failure, machines vs free weights and "complex periodization" did not consistently change outcomes for the average healthy adult. These are the authors' conclusions (opinion level), not table entries.
  - General health: "At least two days per week at moderate to heavy loads for all major muscle groups".
  - <https://acsm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Pronouncement-ppt-deck_resistance-training-ps.pdf>
- **ACSM 2009 position stand** (Ratamess et al., MSSE 41:687–708; free PDF), summary page:
  - Novices: 8–12RM. "Intermediate" is defined as about 6 months of consistent training.
  - Intermediate to advanced: 1–12RM, periodised with emphasis on heavy 1–6RM, 3–5 min rest.
  - Frequency: 2–3 days/week for novices, 3–4 intermediate, 4–5 advanced.
  - Hypertrophy: 1–12RM with emphasis on 6–12RM, 1–2 min rest, multiple sets.
  - Local muscular endurance: 40–60% 1RM, > 15 reps, < 90 s rest.
  - Order: large muscle groups before small, multi-joint before single-joint, higher intensity before lower.
  - <https://sfu.ca/~ryand/kin343/ACSMresistance.pdf>
- **Schoenfeld, Grgic, Ogborn & Krieger 2017** (JSCR meta-analysis): heavy loads gave larger 1RM gains, and hypertrophy was similar across loads. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/>
- **Currier et al. 2023** (BJSM Bayesian network meta-analysis; 178 strength studies, 119 hypertrophy studies; Phillips senior author): "Higher-load (>80% of single repetition maximum) prescriptions maximised strength gains". All prescriptions promoted hypertrophy comparably, and the top-ranked ones used multiple sets. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106807>
- **Schoenfeld, Grgic, Van Every & Plotkin 2021** (Sports 9:32, open access): heavy loads optimise strength, while hypertrophy can be achieved "across a wide spectrum of loading zones". They propose a looser continuum than the classic one. <https://doaj.org/article/6d51b905504d46f0873bb43d5d84bf21> (DOAJ record; full text not read)
- **Schoenfeld et al. 2015** (JSCR; 18 trained men, 9 per group; 8 weeks). 25–35 reps vs 8–12 reps, to failure:
  - Hypertrophy was similar.
  - Squat 1RM rose +19.6% with heavy loads vs +8.8% with light loads. Bench 1RM showed only a trend (6.5% vs 2.0%).
  - Bench endurance at 50% 1RM improved only with light loads (+16.6% vs −1.2%).
  - This is one small trial, illustrating the meta-analyses above. <https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4037869> (record returned 503 on re-check; figures confirmed from the Europe PMC abstract, doi 10.1519/JSC.0000000000000958)
- **Nunes et al. 2021** (Eur J Sport Sci 21:149–157; 11 studies). Across all strength tests there was no overall order effect. But whichever exercise came first gained more: "increases in muscular strength are the largest in the exercises performed at the beginning of an exercise session". Hypertrophy was similar whatever the order. <https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391.2020.1733672> (abstract via Europe PMC)
- **WHO 2020 guidelines** (Bull et al., BJSM 54:1451), as tabulated by Okely et al. 2021 (a commentary): adults should do muscle-strengthening activities "at moderate or greater intensity" that "involve all major muscle groups" on at least 2 days a week, alongside 150–300 min of moderate aerobic activity. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9219310>. The BJSM page returned 403. The US guidelines say the same, "at least 2 days each week": <https://odphp.health.gov/our-work/nutrition-physical-activity/physical-activity-guidelines/current-guidelines/top-10-things-know>
- **Momma et al. 2022** (BJSM; meta-analysis of 16 prospective cohort studies).
  - Muscle-strengthening activity was linked to 10–17% lower risk of all-cause mortality, cardiovascular disease, total cancer and diabetes.
  - The curves were J-shaped for mortality, cardiovascular disease and cancer, with the largest reduction at about 30–60 min a week. The diabetes curve was L-shaped.
  - This is observational, so it shows association, not cause. It shouldn't be used to cap Ben's training time.
  - <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9209691/>
- **Rønnestad & Mujika 2014** (Scand J Med Sci Sports 24:603–612; narrative review; no funding or conflicts declared). Much of its evidence is the first author's own trials. Its "Practical recommendations" section says:
  - Choose exercises that work the sport's muscles and imitate its movements, lifted with maximal intended concentric speed.
  - 2 sessions a week, as a "daily undulating" programme, for ~12 weeks in the preparatory period: "between 4 RM and 10 RM and 2–3 sets with approximately 2–3 min of rest".
  - Learn technique with lighter loads first.
  - "Commence at low level with the concurrent endurance training during the first 2–3 weeks", because legs feel heavy. Ideally run the block just after the competition season.
  - In season, about 1 low-volume, high-intensity session a week "seems to maintain" the gains (citing Rønnestad 2010b and 2011b).
  - *It gives no RPE cap or failure guidance.* The "stop short of failure" in GA6 is my Practice addition.
  - doi [10.1111/sms.12104](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12104). The journal version is paywalled; I read a third-party copy (pp. 608–609).
- **Llanos-Lagos et al. 2024** (Sports Med meta-analysis; middle- and long-distance runners): high-load strength training gave a small improvement in running economy (ES −0.27), larger at higher speeds. Submaximal-load and isometric training didn't beat controls. <https://repository.essex.ac.uk/37272/>
- **Rønnestad, Hansen & Raastad 2010** (Eur J Appl Physiol 110:1269–1282; cyclists, n = 6 per group): 2 sessions a week for 12 weeks, then 1 a week for the first 13 weeks of the competition period, kept the gains in thigh cross-sectional area and 1RM. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-010-1622-4>. The repository copy was unreachable on re-check (DNS failure); the abstract was confirmed via search summaries and LIDA.
- **Rønnestad, Nymark & Raastad 2011** (JSCR 25:2653–2660; professional soccer, n = 7 per group): 1 session a week in season kept strength and 40 m sprint gains for 12 weeks; 1 session every 2 weeks lost them. <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/21873897> (abstract via Europe PMC)
- Maintenance (Bickel 2011; Spiering 2021) and volume bands (Pelland 2025; V4) are summarised in [volume-by-frequency.md](volume-by-frequency.md) §1 and §5, and in §6 below.

**Inference:** the classic "rep continuum" bands (NSCA-style 30–90 s rest for hypertrophy, for example) are older than the evidence above. The profiles use the newer findings: heavy loads for strength, a wide rep range for muscle, longer rests on compounds (E10), and the two health guidelines for general fitness.

### 3. How training is run

- **Days a week and session length** are covered in V2–V7. Frequency sets how many hard sets fit; it doesn't change the goal.
  - **Fact:** Pelland 2025 found the biggest per-lift strength step was from 1 to 2 sessions a week, with little difference for hypertrophy once volume is equal. Schoenfeld 2019 also found no meaningful hypertrophy effect of volume-equated frequency.
- **Short sessions.**
  - Iversen 2021 (a narrative review on time efficiency) gives a minimum of 4 weekly sets per muscle at 6–15RM, and says weekly volume matters more than frequency.
  - Zhang 2025 (meta-analysis): supersets shorten sessions without compromising volume or chronic gains. The ~37% time saving is from the full text (per V); the abstract gives no figure. Same-muscle supersets reduced volume load, and supersets raised perceived exertion.
  - Spiering 2021: keeping the load maintains strength.
  - **Inference:** GA10's priority lists put each goal's highest-value work first, so brief scenario 4 ("20 minutes left") keeps it.
- **Other training.**
  - **Fact (HIIT and classes).** Sabag et al. 2018 (J Sports Sci meta-analysis; HIIT + resistance training vs resistance training alone):
    - Lower-body strength gains were slightly smaller (ES −0.25). Hypertrophy and upper-body strength were similar.
    - The lower-body deficit appeared mainly with *cycling* HIIT (ES −0.38, p = 0.07); running HIIT showed no significant effect.
    - The authors suggest longer rest between modalities may help. A conference version reported no difference when sessions were > 24 h apart (secondary report; not in the journal abstract).
    - <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29658408/>
  - **Fact (spacing).** Robineau et al. 2016 (JSCR; 58 amateur rugby players; 7 weeks): strength gains were smaller with 0 h between aerobic and strength sessions than with 6 h or 24 h. The authors advise ≥ 6 h. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25546450/>
  - **Fact (field sports).** Silva et al. 2018 (Sports Med; 77 studies, 1,105 players): hamstring force (ES −0.7), physical performance (2–4%), creatine kinase, soreness and well-being were still impaired 72 h after a soccer match. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29098658/>
  - **Fact (muscle damage and running).** Burt et al. 2023 (Sci Sports meta-analysis): exercise-induced muscle damage raised the physiological cost of high-intensity running at 24 and 48 h. <https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/77226/>
  - **Opinion.** Wang & Bo 2024 (Medicine; narrative review; no conflicts declared):
    - Limit endurance work to "no more than 3 days per week" when strength gains matter.
    - Cycling may interfere less than running.
    - Lifting first is one option.
    - Leave 6–24 h between modalities.
    - <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11688070/> (Weak: a narrative review. Its cycling claim conflicts with Sabag 2018 for HIIT; see §10.)
  - **Inference:** GA11's four-way split lets classes and sport use the rules already approved for endurance (11–12) without new special cases. Low-intensity activity has no evidence of interference.
- **Equipment.**
  - **Fact:** Haugen 2023 (13 studies, 1,016 participants): free-weight training gave larger gains on free-weight strength tests. Machine training showed only a non-significant trend on machine tests (p = 0.064). Hypertrophy was similar (5 studies).
  - **Fact:** Kassiano 2025 (Sports Med; 9 studies): unilateral vs bilateral training gave similar hypertrophy, and strength followed "the principle of specificity".
  - **Fact:** ACSM 2026: machines vs free weights didn't consistently change outcomes.
  - **Inference:** this is GA12. Swapping equipment costs little for muscle and health goals, but it is a real trade-off for a strength goal on that specific lift.
- **Experience.**
  - **Fact:** Steele et al. 2017 (PeerJ; one study, n = 141, own training loads): the least experienced lifters under-predicted reps to failure by about 4–5, the most experienced by about 1–2. "RIR should be used cautiously". <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5712461/>
  - **Fact:** Halperin et al. 2022 (Sports Med exploratory meta-analysis; 12 studies, 414 participants):
    - On average, lifters under-predicted reps to failure by about 1 rep.
    - Accuracy was better with fewer reps to failure and worse above 12 reps.
    - "Participants' training status did not seem to influence prediction accuracy".
    - <https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01559-x>
    - So experience-based distrust of RPE is **Weak and contested**. The robust point is the > 12 reps caveat (E5).
  - **Fact:** Moesgaard et al. 2022 (Sports Med; 35 studies; volume-equated):
    - Periodised training gave more 1RM gain than non-periodised overall (ES 0.31).
    - Undulating beat linear only in trained participants (ES 0.61); untrained showed no difference.
    - There was no hypertrophy effect.
    - <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044672/> (abstract via Europe PMC)
  - **Disagreement:** ACSM 2009 suggests 8–12RM for novices, while popular novice programmes (Starting Strength, StrongLifts, GZCLP; see P) use about 5 reps. Both work. GA13's 5–8 is Practice.

### 4. The endurance switch (rules 11–13)

**Inference / Practice.** The approved rules are about real sessions: same-day order, spacing, protecting a key session, and a heavy-strength floor. They only make sense when such a session exists. The switch is therefore based on data, not on a goal label:

1. **Collect** GA11 (a)–(c) sessions:
   - planned in the next 7 days (`ScheduledSession`);
   - imported in the last 7 days (`EnduranceActivity`, with its source label);
   - reported by Ben in chat or check-in;
   - or Ben's stated regular other training (Your data, with provenance). Its stated days count as planned, labelled "usual"; the coach confirms them when planning.
2. **None found** → rules 11–13 are dormant. The coach doesn't mention interference, spacing or key sessions.
3. **Found** → rule 11 for same-day plans, rule 12 for spacing and the 48 h protection of key sessions (72 h after a match, GA11 c), and rule 13's floor (GA6/GA7; decision 1).
4. **Key sessions** are what Ben marks. The coach may *suggest* defaults (GA15) but never overwrites a planned run, ride or match (brief scenario 3).
5. **Data honesty:** fatigue is never inferred from a session that isn't planned, imported or reported, or from a "usual" session Ben didn't do. Strength and endurance load stay separate metrics (C8; rule 6).

**Fact (tapering):**
- **Travis et al. 2020** (Sports; powerlifting).
  - A PRISMA systematic review of 16 studies, without a meta-analysis. The abstract notes that "evidence regarding peaking protocols for strength and power athletes is lacking".
  - Its conclusions:
    - Taper over 1–2 weeks, reducing volume by about 30–70%. The discussion found ~30–50% cuts best.
    - "Maintain training intensity ≥85% 1RM or reduce training intensity" slightly.
    - A 2–7-day training cessation is acceptable; longer breaks risk detraining.
- **Bosquet et al. 2007** (MSSE meta-analysis; 27 studies, mostly endurance): the best taper was ~2 weeks, cutting volume 41–60% exponentially while keeping intensity and frequency.
- **Wang et al. 2023** (PLOS ONE meta-analysis; 14 studies, endurance athletes): tapers of ≤ 21 days, cutting volume 41–60% while keeping intensity and frequency, improved time-trial performance. It has no resistance-training data.
- **Inference:**
  - Cutting *lifting* volume by 40–60% before an endurance event borrows those figures.
  - A mixed event gets the overlap: about 40–50% less volume over 1–2 weeks with intensity kept.

### 6. Goal changes over time

**Fact (why sequencing and maintenance work):**
- **Bickel, Cross & Bamman 2011** (MSSE RCT; 70 adults). After 16 weeks of training 3×/week, a ⅓ or even 1/9 dose for 32 weeks kept muscle size in 20–35-year-olds but not in 60–75-year-olds. Strength gains were largely kept even by the group that stopped training.
- **Spiering et al. 2021** (JSCR narrative review): strength and size are kept for up to 32 weeks with 1 session a week and 1 set per exercise if relative load is kept. Older adults may need 2 sessions and 2–3 sets. "Exercise intensity seems to be the key variable".
- **Rønnestad 2010 and 2011:** 1 session a week in a competition period kept the gains (above).
- **McMaster et al. 2013** (Sports Med; 27 studies, elite rugby union, rugby league and American football): "Strength levels can be maintained for up to 3 weeks of detraining", after which decay speeds up. Mean strength loss was 14.5% over 7.2 ± 5.8 weeks of detraining. <https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2013/788>. *Correction:* an earlier draft called the 3-week figure "unverified"; it is in the abstract.

**Fact (why ask rather than impose):** Ntoumanis et al. 2021 (Health Psychol Rev; meta-analysis of 73 self-determination-theory interventions): autonomy- and need-supportive approaches gave small-to-medium, varied improvements in health behaviour. <https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-self-determination-theory-informed-interventio/> (abstract only). This is indirect, supporting context only. The binding reasons are CLAUDE.md rules 2 and 10 and the brief's "don't silently infer lasting goals".

**Opinion:** block periodisation (Issurin) concentrates on a few qualities per block and relies on "residual" effects to hold the rest. The residual-duration figures I found (e.g. ~30 days for maximal strength) came only from secondary sites, so they're not used as rules. Issurin 2008: <https://coachsci.sdsu.edu/csa/vol161/issurin.htm>

4. If Ben changes goals again before applying the plan, the plan proposal is stale and rejected (rule 5).

### 8. Worked examples (illustrative, not Ben's data)

- **A. Strength, 3 days × 60 min, no other training.**
  - Rules 11–13 stay dormant.
  - Squat, bench and deadlift/press are done first each day at 1–6 reps, RPE 7–9, with 2 exposures of each lift a week (heavy/medium/light rotation; V-example B).
  - Accessories 6–12 reps; rest 3–5 min on heavy main-lift sets.
- **B. Same person, who reports Tuesday and Saturday runs, with Saturday long.**
  - The switch turns on.
  - Friday's heavy lower session conflicts with rule 12 (within 48 h of the long run). The coach proposes moving it to Wednesday, or swapping Friday to upper body, and shows the trade-off.
  - Saturday is key only if Ben marks it. With a Strength focus, the coach suggests it as key but doesn't assume it.
- **C. Endurance (cyclist), 2 × 45 min lifting days; the race season starts next month.**
  - A future-scope proposal: from the season start, 1 heavy lower-body session a week (2–3 sets of 4–6 reps on squat or leg press, plus calf and trunk work).
  - Upper body is kept per "also matters" (if set).
  - Needs decision 1 approved; otherwise 2 lighter sessions.
- **D. Ben adds "obstacle race, 10 weeks".**
  - The coach asks what it involves and whether it's the priority. Ben describes running plus carries, climbs and crawls, and says yes.
  - Mapping: Endurance plus practice of the event's movements.
  - Weeks 1–2: build. Weeks 3–8: weekly carries and grip work at race-like loads, plus 1–2 heavy sessions.
  - Weeks 9–10: taper (−40–60% lifting volume, loads kept).
  - Every phase change is a proposal and applies only after Ben accepts it.
- **E. Muscle → Strength after a 12-week block.**
  - Goal proposal, then plan proposal.
  - Main lifts move to the start of each session and shift towards 3–6 reps over 2 weeks.
  - Muscle work drops to the "also matters" floor (≥ 6 sets per named group) or ⅓ of recent.
  - The session budget is recomputed, because heavy sets take ~4 min each (V-example B). If fewer sets now fit, the coach says so.
- **F. The event date passes.**
  - Next coach turn: "How did the race go? Keep Endurance as your focus, or go back to Strength?"
  - Nothing changes until Ben answers and applies the change.

### 9. Guardrail notes (CLAUDE.md, ADR 0006)

- **No guardrail is overridden.** Profiles change targets, not the load limits.
  - Every load is snapped to an achievable value (rounded down after an overshoot) and kept within equipment min/max.
  - "≥ 80%" and "≥ 85% 1RM" are computed from an estimated 1RM that is labelled as such. Snapping down may land slightly below the band, and that's acceptable.
  - A pain flag blocks increases for that movement whatever the goal. The coach doesn't diagnose (rule 9) and doesn't use swaps to "work around" pain unless Ben asks.
  - Completed sets are never edited.
- **Rule 2/3:**
  - Goal changes, phase changes, tapers and rebuilt plans for future sessions are always proposals with before/after, Apply and Undo. Event phase targets don't take effect on their own.
  - When Ben asks, today's remaining sets apply straight away with Undo. When the coach raises a change itself, it only offers it.
  - GA16 limits unprompted goal questions to four signals, so the coach doesn't nag. This is still a new coach-initiated behaviour (decision 8).
  - Switching "too easy" off in a taper only removes offers (decision 7).
- **Rule 5:** goal and plan proposals carry version IDs. A plan proposal built on an older goal version is rejected.
- **Rule 6:**
  - Endurance and sport fatigue are used only for planned, imported or reported sessions; "usual" sessions are confirmed before they're relied on. No recovery scores.
  - Estimated 1RM is labelled as an estimate.
- **Rule 10:**
  - The profiles, floors, event mappings and taper numbers are **Coaching knowledge**.
  - Ben's goals, events, other training, key-session marks and experience are **Your data**, with provenance and dates.
  - A general rule Ben states in chat becomes a playbook candidate, not a personal goal.
- **ADR 0006 fallback (no AI):**
  - The built-in rule doesn't change goals or plans.
  - Whether its default rep ranges should vary by goal is an open question. Today they are set by exercise category (provisional decisions).

### 10. Where sources disagree

- **Rest for hypertrophy:** ACSM 2009 says 1–2 min; newer meta-analyses (Singer 2024, E10) slightly favour longer rest on compounds. The profiles use 2–3 min on compounds and 1–2 min on isolation, so both fit when time is short. For strength, ACSM 2009's 3–5 min is used only for heavy top sets, to stay within approved rule 9 (2–3 min, up to 5).
- **How often to lift in an endurance season:**
  - Rule 13's "≥ 2" comes from the general concurrent-training literature (C5).
  - The "1 a week maintains" result comes from two small trials (n = 6–7 per group) in trained athletes.
  - Both are plausible. The difference is the goal: building or keeping strength while lifting is primary, versus holding strength while endurance is primary (decision 1).
- **Periodisation:** Moesgaard 2022 finds a small 1RM benefit for periodised training overall, plus an undulating-over-linear benefit only in trained lifters. ACSM 2026 says complex periodisation didn't consistently matter for average adults. The populations and comparisons differ, which is why GA13 adds variation only for experienced lifters with a strength goal.
- **Muscular endurance as a goal:** ACSM 2009 recommends light loads, > 15 reps and short rest. ACSM 2026 found too little data to say which prescription enhances it beyond normal training. One small trial (Schoenfeld 2015) favours light loads for that specific test. So it's handled as event-specific practice (GA8), not a sixth profile.
- **Does experience change RPE accuracy?** Steele 2017 (one study) says novices are off by 4–5 reps. Halperin 2022 (meta-analysis) found training status made no clear difference, with an average error of about 1 rep that grew above 12 reps. The meta-analysis is preferred, so GA13 doesn't depend on it.
- **Cycling vs running interference:**
  - Wang & Bo 2024 (narrative review) and older reviews suggest cycling interferes less.
  - Sabag 2018 found the HIIT lower-body deficit mainly with cycling.
  - The modes, intensities and outcomes differ, so the playbook doesn't prefer one mode. It spaces by session type and intensity (rule 12, GA11).
- **Novice rep range:** ACSM 2009 uses 8–12RM; 5-rep novice programmes (P) also work. GA13's 5–8 is a middle ground (Practice).

### 13. Fact-check log (2026-10-09)

Each cited claim was re-checked against its source (abstract, free text or deck). Changes from the first draft:

**Corrected source readings**
- **Moesgaard 2022:** the "mainly in trained lifters" finding applies to undulating vs linear, not to periodised vs non-periodised.
- **McMaster 2013:** the "3 weeks retained" figure *is* in the abstract. The population is elite rugby and American football players, not "field-sport athletes" in general.
- **Rønnestad & Mujika 2014:**
  - It doesn't give "RPE 7–8.5", "never to failure" or "moving towards 4–6RM". These are now labelled Practice, or removed.
  - Its 2–3 easier weeks refer to the *endurance* side, not the lifting.
- **Momma 2022:** the J-shape applies to mortality, cardiovascular disease and cancer only; diabetes is L-shaped.

**Lowered grades**
- **Steele 2017:** "novices misjudge by 4–5 reps" is a single study, contradicted by the Halperin 2022 meta-analysis. GA13's grade drops from Moderate to Weak/contested, and the conflict 2 reasoning is corrected.
- **Exercise order** (GA2): from Strong to Moderate. The source is one meta-analysis, which found no overall effect, only that whichever exercise comes first gains more.
- **GA6:** split into Moderate (2×/week build), Weak (1×/week maintenance) and Practice (RPE cap).
- **Travis 2020:** a systematic review without a meta-analysis, whose authors say the evidence is lacking. Now graded Weak–Moderate.
- **Endurance-taper figures:** these come from endurance training, so applying them to lifting volume is labelled Practice.
- **Muscle ≥ 2×/week** (GA3): Practice, because volume-equated frequency adds little for size.

**Added caveats**
- **Sabag 2018:** the lower-body deficit appeared mainly with cycling HIIT, which conflicts with Wang & Bo's "cycling interferes less".
- **Age:** older adults may need ≥ 2 sessions a week to maintain (Bickel, Spiering).

**Consistency with approved rules**
- Strength rest aligned with rule 9 (2–5 min, with 3–5 on heavy top sets).
- Deload wording aligned with rule 10 (RPE ≤ 7).

**Guardrails**
- Event phase targets now apply only through an applied proposal.
- "Usual" endurance sessions are confirmed before they're relied on (rule 6).
- Decisions 7–10 added.
