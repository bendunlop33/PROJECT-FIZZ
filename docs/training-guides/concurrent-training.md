*Training-researcher survey for Ben. All sources checked 2026-10-08. Not medical advice.*

**Summary**
1. Pooled data show little or no interference with strength or muscle growth for most people. The exception is lower-body maximal strength in trained lifters when endurance work is done in the same session (< ~20 min apart). Grade: Moderate–Strong.
2. Interference is local and dose-dependent. Running interferes more than cycling, and more endurance frequency/duration means more risk. Grade: Moderate.
3. If both are in one session and strength matters, lift first. Separate heavy legs and hard/long endurance by ≥ 6 h, ideally a day. Order doesn't change VO2max gains. Grade: Moderate.
4. Hard lower-body work (muscle damage) worsens high-intensity running for 24–48 h and easy running at 48 h. Keep heavy leg sessions away from key long runs/rides. Heavy strength work *improves* running economy. Grade: Moderate.
5. HYROX is mostly running (≈ 60% of race time). Finish time tracks aerobic fitness, not maximal strength (one small study). Train it as endurance-first plus strength-endurance and station practice. Grade: Weak (sport-specific data are thin).

## Details

### 1. Is there an interference effect?

**Documented fact**
- **Wilson et al. 2012** (JSCR; 21 studies, 422 effect sizes): concurrent running, but not cycling, significantly reduced hypertrophy and strength gains. Endurance frequency and duration correlated negatively with strength/hypertrophy/power outcomes. <https://www.academia.edu/103997237/Concurrent_Training> (DOI 10.1519/JSC.0b013e31823a3e2d; checked 2026-10-08)
- **Petré et al. 2021** (Sports Med; 27 studies): lower-body 1RM was impaired in *trained* people (ES −0.35), not in moderately trained (−0.20, ns) or untrained people (0.03). The impairment appeared when modes were < 20 min apart, not > 2 h apart. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8053170/>
- **Schumann et al. 2022** (Sports Med; 43 studies, 1,090 participants): no interference for maximal strength or whole-muscle hypertrophy, across modality, frequency, training status and timing. Possible small type I fibre-level effect with running. Explosive strength may be attenuated, especially in same-session designs. <https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/71546/1/Schumann2021_Article_CompatibilityOfConcurrentAerob.pdf>

**Why sources disagree:** Wilson (2012) pooled older studies with higher endurance doses. Schumann (2022) pooled more and newer studies, mostly in untrained/moderately trained people. Petré split by training status and found the effect only in trained lifters. **Inference:** Ben is a trained lifter, so treat interference as small but real for lower-body maximal strength, and manage it with timing (C1–C3) rather than avoiding endurance.

### 2. Session order and spacing

- **Eddens et al. 2018** (Sports Med; 10 studies): resistance-first gave better lower-body dynamic strength (+6.9%). No order effect on hypertrophy, static strength, VO2max or body fat. <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-017-0784-1>
- **Murlasits et al. 2018** (J Sports Sci): strength-first gave +3.96 kg lower-body 1RM. Order didn't affect aerobic capacity. <https://busqueda.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/es/mdl-28783467>
- **Gao & Yu 2023** (Front Physiol; 19 RCTs): strength-first was slightly better for knee extension/flexion strength (SMD 0.19). No VO2max difference. <https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1072679/full>
- **Robineau et al. 2016** (JSCR; 58 rugby players, 7 wk): with 0 h between sessions, strength gains were lower than with 6 h, 24 h or strength-only. VO2peak improved most with 24 h spacing. The authors advise ≥ 6 h between contrasting sessions. <https://hal-univ-bourgogne.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01409685>

### 3. Scheduling around a key long run/ride

- **Burt, Doma & Connor 2023** (Science & Sports; meta-analysis): after muscle-damaging exercise, soreness and strength loss are present at 24 and 48 h. Running economy at low intensity is unchanged at 24 h but worse at 48 h. High-intensity running is worse at both 24 and 48 h. <https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/77226/>
- **Inference / Practice:** a "hard days hard, easy days easy" week fits these data. Heavy legs + intervals/tempo on the same day (lift first, or ≥ 6 h apart), then an easy or upper-body day, and keep heavy lower-body work ≥ 48 h before the long run/ride. Cycling is concentric-dominant and less damaging than running, so a long ride is somewhat less sensitive (Wilson modality finding; Weak).
- **Example week (illustrative only, not a prescription):** Mon lower heavy + easy spin; Tue easy run; Wed upper; Thu intervals (am) + lower-body light/accessory (pm); Fri rest or upper; Sat long run/ride; Sun easy/rest.

### 4. Strength helps endurance

- **Llanos-Lagos et al. 2024** (Sports Med; meta-analysis): high-load strength training (ES −0.27), plyometrics and combined methods improve running economy in middle/long-distance runners. Submaximal-load and isometric work were less effective. Certainty was moderate. <https://repository.essex.ac.uk/37272/>

### 5. HYROX-specific

**Format (documented):** 8 × 1 km run, each followed by a station: SkiErg 1,000 m, sled push 50 m, sled pull 50 m, burpee broad jumps 80 m, row 1,000 m, farmers carry 200 m, sandbag lunges 100 m, wall balls 100 reps. Loads vary by division. Official rulebook: <https://maintain.hyrox.com/rulebooks/HYROX_RulebookSingles_EN.pdf> (not read in full; station specs via <https://fitnessvolt.com/roxrace/stations/>, secondary).

**Evidence**
- **Brandt et al. 2025** (Front Physiol; n = 11 recreational athletes, simulated race): ~80% of race time at "very hard" HR intensity. Runs took about 59% of finish time (median 51 of 86.5 min). Highest lactate at wall balls. Faster finish times correlated with VO2max (ρ −0.71), endurance training volume (ρ −0.68) and lower body fat. Grip strength and resistance-training volume showed no correlation. The authors call HYROX endurance-focused, with moderate-to-low maximal-strength demands, and recommend concurrent-training principles. Limits: tiny sample, solo race, non-official sled. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11994925/>
- **HYROX performance review** (Strength & Conditioning Journal, 2025): aerobic capacity is foundational. Anaerobic capacity, local muscular endurance and strength/power matter for stations (sleds). Sport-specific research is limited. <https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4092970> (index record; full text not read)
- **HYROX organiser advice** (commercial source; older page): build running, train movement standards, try the sled before race day, run after stations to simulate the race. <https://hyrox.com/best-hyrox-preparation/>. HYROX also publishes a free Sports Science Report (Dec 2025). <https://hyrox.com/the-science-of-hyrox/>

**Expert opinion / commercial:** Athletica (a brief competitor) markets running planned around leg fatigue from strength, plus station-specific intensity. <https://www.athletica.ai/sports/hyrox>. Official partner programmes are covered in `programmes.md`.

*(Superseded 2026-10-09 by playbook rule G; kept for the record.)* **Inference for Ben:** if Ben's priority is HYROX performance, the evidence says running volume and aerobic fitness come first. Keep heavy strength at maintenance-to-moderate volume (2 sessions/week), add strength-endurance and station practice, and use C1–C3 to protect the key run sessions. If his priority is strength with HYROX as a side goal, flip the emphasis and accept slower race progress. The coach should ask which is primary, not assume.
