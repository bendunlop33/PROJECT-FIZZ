## 3. H0 — the central test, answered

> **H0: is Waterdrop's light dosing an oversight, or a deliberate choice?**

### **VERDICT: DELIBERATE. Unambiguously so. The proposed gap is a trap in the form it was framed.**

Four FACTs carry it, all from Waterdrop's own GB pages read on 2026-09-22:

**1. The cube mass is effectively constant across the whole range, and the vitamin load varies roughly 18-fold across it.**

| Line | Cube mass | Declared vitamin mass per serving (sum of declared vitamins) |
|---|---|---|
| ICE TEA Peach | 2.1 g | ~14.4 mg (2 nutrients, both at exactly 15% NRV) |
| Microdrink Blackcurrant | 2.0 g | ~20.6 mg (3 nutrients, 25–45% NRV) |
| Microdrink Lemon | 2.1 g | ~25.9 mg (7 nutrients, all 25% NRV) |
| Microenergy White Peach | 2.1 g | ~51.9 mg (6 nutrients, all 50% NRV) + 90 mg caffeine |
| Microlyte Berry | 2.2 g | ~95.9 mg (9 nutrients, 20–100% NRV) **plus 298 mg of declared elemental minerals** |

ESTIMATE, method shown: each figure is the arithmetic sum of the declared per-serving amounts in §2.1, converting µg to mg. It is a mass of declared nutrients, not of raw material — the salt forms weigh considerably more.

A 2.0 g cube and a 2.2 g cube are the same object for formulation purposes. Waterdrop puts 14 mg of vitamins in one and 96 mg of vitamins plus ~300 mg of minerals in the other. **Mass is not what is deciding the dose.**

**2. Waterdrop's own ingredient ordering proves the cube can carry a heavy mineral load.** Microlyte Berry's declared ingredients run: citric acid, **potassium chloride, calcium phosphates, magnesium carbonate**, sodium carbonate, natural flavouring, vitamin C… (https://en.waterdrop.com/products/microlyte-berry). Three mineral salts sit at positions 2–4 by mass, ahead of the flavour and the sweetener, inside a 2.2 g cube. ESTIMATE: at 120 mg calcium and 56.3 mg magnesium, the carbonate/phosphate salts alone are several hundred milligrams — a large fraction of the cube. **The effervescent base is not consuming 50–75% of a Waterdrop cube.** This narrows, though does not overturn, A-019's and A-066's "50–75% acid and carbonates" premise for this specific format.

**3. Waterdrop is dosing to regulatory thresholds, not to nothing.** ICE TEA sits at **exactly 15% NRV on exactly two nutrients** — the precise minimum for a "source of" nutrition claim and therefore for any authorised health claim (A-009). Microdrink Lemon sits at exactly 25% on seven. Microenergy at exactly 50% on six. Microlyte at exactly 50% on five and 15% on four minerals. **Nobody hits 15.0%, 25.0% and 50.0% repeatedly by accident.** This is a deliberate, tiered claims-and-cost architecture.

**4. Microlyte is positioned separately, and it already is the product being proposed.** It is a 2.2 g cube carrying nine vitamins and four electrolytes, with vitamin C at 100% NRV and the B-complex at 50%, sold in five flavours at £10.99 / 12 on the GB store, with an explicit benefit name (RECHARGE) and printed occasions (Exercise, Travel, Heat & Sun, Rapid Hydration). A-067 flagged exactly this risk. **It is confirmed.**

### CSO verification, 2026-09-22 — and it is stronger than the verdict above states

I re-read Microlyte Berry and Microdrink Lemon directly to test this verdict independently. Both panels reproduce exactly as recorded. **But the Microlyte panel contains the decisive detail, and it is not the vitamin ladder — it is the minerals.**

**FACT.** Microlyte Berry declares **magnesium at 56.3 mg** per 500 ml serving (https://en.waterdrop.com/products/microlyte-berry, read 2026-09-22).

**FACT.** The GB NHC register conditions "Magnesium contributes to electrolyte balance" on a minimum of **56.25 mg** of magnesium. `research/opportunity-areas.md` §2.4 identifies that single claim as the *only* authorised wording in the entire register that comes near the hydration concept for a zero-sugar enhancer.

**Waterdrop is dosed to 56.3 mg against a 56.25 mg threshold. That is a 0.09% margin.** Nobody lands there by formulating for efficacy, or for taste, or for cost. They landed there by reading the register and dosing to the condition.

**FACT, corroborating.** The product page then prints the claims it has bought: *"Magnesium contributes to electrolyte balance. Vitamin C, B6, B12, niacin and pantothenic acid contribute to the reduction of tiredness of fatigue. Calcium and magnesium contribute to normal muscle function… Pantothenic acid contributes to normal mental performance. Chloride contributes to normal digestion by production of hydrochloric acid in the stomach."*

**OPINION, and it is the finding that should govern the decision.** `research/opportunity-areas.md` identified the green territories a 2.0 g cube can lawfully occupy — tiredness and fatigue via the nine-nutrient claim, "focus" via the single pantothenic acid sentence, electrolyte balance via magnesium at 56.25 mg. **Microlyte already occupies every one of them, and prints them on the page.** The four mineral figures sitting at exactly 15% NRV — the "source of" threshold, the cheapest point at which any claim becomes legal — say the same thing a second time.

So the conclusion is not merely that Waterdrop chose its dose. It is that **Waterdrop has already reverse-engineered its formulation from the GB claims register, and arrived at the answer this project was working towards.** The whitespace we were testing for is occupied by the incumbent, deliberately, and defended by the same arithmetic that would govern any entrant.

### What that does and does not kill

**It kills:** "Waterdrop has left the vitamin-led cube unoccupied." It has not. The stronger cube exists, it is Waterdrop's, and it is on sale in the UK.

**It does not kill, and this is the residual:** even Microlyte does not reach full NRV on anything except vitamin C, and holds magnesium, calcium and chloride at 15%. **FACT:** no dry format found in this exercise at ~2 g delivers full-NRV magnesium (375 mg) or calcium (800 mg), and the heavier formats do not either — Phizz at 4.3 g holds magnesium at 15%, High5 at 7 g at 40%, Revive Active at 15%. So A-066's arithmetic stands for the bulky minerals **at every mass in this category, not just at 2 g.** The practical envelope for a ~2 g cube is: **vitamin C to 100% NRV, B-complex to 50%, bulky minerals to 15–20%** — which is precisely where Microlyte already sits. **OPINION: Microlyte is at or very near the format's practical ceiling. There is no meaningful dosing headroom above it inside a Waterdrop-sized cube.**

**Consequence for the proposition, OPINION:** a "more functional, more vitamin-led Waterdrop cube" is not a positioning gap, it is a **sixth step on a ladder Waterdrop has already climbed**, and the top rung is taken. Any differentiation must come from something other than dose — which is, in fact, what the 2026-09-22 taste-and-ease decision already said. This finding **supports** that decision and **removes** the "more vitamins than Waterdrop" argument entirely, including the softened version at A-022.

## 5. Where the varied-benefit dry range already exists in the UK

**FACT.** The "Vitamin Well benefit architecture, executed dry, sold D2C in the UK" is **not** unoccupied. **Zooki** runs exactly that, today: a Hydration+ sachet range with four named benefit variants — **Skin Radiance, Energy & Focus, Exercise & Recover, Liver Health** — plus a base electrolyte sachet, sold D2C at zooki.com with a GBP storefront and per-serving pricing shown on the page (https://zooki.com/products/hydration-energy-and-focus, read 2026-09-22). Each variant carries a distinct hero active at a visible dose (caffeine 100 mg + L-theanine 200 mg; L-glutamine 1,000 mg; choline 500 mg + NAC 600 mg + silymarin 300 mg; Damasty® 150 mg) and hangs authorised vitamin claims off vitamin C, B6, B12, zinc, magnesium and selenium.

**FACT.** Berocca now also runs a benefit-named effervescent range in GB — **Energy, Immuno, Mind** — with genuinely different panels (Mind adds iron 12 mg, magnesium 135 mg and Spanish sage oil 25 µl; Immuno drops the B-complex almost entirely and loads C 1000 mg, E 45 mg, A, D, selenium, iron, copper).

**OPINION.** The combination the question treats as whitespace — dry format + varied named benefits + D2C + UK — has at least two live occupants (Zooki in sachet, Waterdrop in cube across three lines) and one large retail occupant (Berocca in tablet). What is genuinely unoccupied is narrower still: **a varied-benefit range in the specific 2 g cube form factor from anyone other than Waterdrop.**

## 6. Is anyone copying the cube form factor in the UK?

**FACT — no.** Searches for UK cube-format entrants returned only Waterdrop itself, plus tablet and sachet brands. The Holland & Barrett product sitemap contains no cube product. The only non-Waterdrop "cube" hits were US listings and a Waterdrop stockist page at Urban Outfitters.

**OPINION, and it cuts both ways.** Four years after Waterdrop launched Microlyte (2022 trade coverage), **no GB brand has copied the cube.** Either the form factor is defensible and hard to make — or it is unattractive to make, because a compressed 2 g cube is a manufacturing constraint that buys you nothing a 4 g tablet or a 5 g sachet does not already buy, at more cost per serving. This is the graveyard question the brief's W6 was going to ask, and the honest reading is that we do not yet know which. **What we can say is that the absence of copycats is not, on its own, evidence of opportunity.**

## 8. The most vitamin-loaded dry format on the UK market

**Stated plainly, as the brief requires.**

**FACT — by breadth of declared nutrients in a unit whose mass is published: High5 Zero+ Electrolytes & Vitamins, a 7 g sachet, carrying 22 declared nutrients** (12 vitamins including K2 75 µg at 100% NRV and D3 25 µg at 500%, plus 10 minerals including iodine, chromium, selenium and manganese), most of them at 100–500% NRV. Sixteen of the 22 sit at or above 100% NRV. £15.00 for 16 sachets at Holland & Barrett.

**FACT — by declared micronutrient mass in a single unit: Holland & Barrett Vitamin C, Vitamin B & Zinc effervescent tablet, ~1.09 g of declared vitamins and minerals per tablet** (vitamin C 1000 mg at 1250% NRV plus the full B-complex and zinc 10 mg), and **Berocca Immuno, ~1.07 g per tablet** (vitamin C 1000 mg, vitamin E 45 mg, zinc 10 mg, iron 5 mg, selenium 110 µg, A, D, B6, B12, folic acid, copper). ESTIMATE, method: arithmetic sum of the declared per-tablet amounts in §2.2, µg converted to mg. **Neither brand publishes a tablet mass**, so neither can be mass-normalised.

**FACT — the most vitamin-loaded CUBE on the UK market is Waterdrop Microlyte BERRY RECHARGE: a 2.2 g cube carrying nine vitamins and four minerals, vitamin C at 100% NRV, five B vitamins at 50% NRV.** ESTIMATE: ~96 mg of declared vitamins plus ~298 mg of declared elemental minerals, i.e. roughly **18% of the cube's mass is declared micronutrient** — the same order as Phizz's 4.3 g tablet (~25%) and High5's 7 g sachet (~20%).

**OPINION.** That last line is the most useful number in this document. It says the cube is **not** a handicapped format on a percentage basis — Waterdrop has already pushed it to the same micronutrient density as the heavy tablets and sachets. It also says there is no room to be twice as good, because the unit is a third of the mass. Anyone proposing a cube must accept the absolute ceiling that follows from 2 g, and must stop arguing about dose.

## 9. Gaps and next research

1. **Supermarket and Boots own-label effervescents** — the whole grocery own-label tier is missing because six retailer domains 403'd. It matters: Boots and Tesco own-label are the price-and-claims floor the brief's Q4 rests on (A-009). Next step: read the pack images, or use a non-blocked wholesaler listing.
2. **Phizz Daily Immune+ numeric panel** — Phizz is the closest GB analogue to a serious vitamin-led hydration tablet and its newest SKU is unreadable. Next step: the pack shot, or ASDA's mobile API.
3. **Tablet masses for Berocca, Vitabiotics and H&B own-label** — without these, the mass-normalised comparison in §8 covers only six products. Next step: net weight from a pack photo (tube net weight ÷ tablet count).
4. **Nuun, Voost, Wellgard, Bulk, MyProtein, Solgar** — GB availability and panels unread. None is likely to change the H0 verdict; all would thicken the tablet and sachet rings.
5. **Zooki and Humantra numeric panels** — both are D2C-first UK brands and both hide their panels. Zooki in particular is the closest live analogue to the proposition and deserves a proper read.
6. **Whether any brand has withdrawn a cube in GB** — the graveyard check at W6 is not done. The absence of copycats (§6) is currently unexplained, and it is the single most important unanswered question about the format.
