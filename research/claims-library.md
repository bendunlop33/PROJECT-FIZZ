## 3. Claims library — vitamins and minerals (the authorised territory)

### 3.1 The two gates every vitamin/mineral claim must pass

1. **Amount gate.** The claim may be used only if the product is at least a **"source of"** that nutrient — **≥15% of the NRV** per portion recommended for daily consumption. "High in" requires **≥30% NRV**. For food supplements the reference is the daily portion, not 100 g. `[V]`
2. **Wording gate.** Only the register's authorised wording, or wording with **the same meaning for the consumer**, may be used. Conditions of use and any mandatory accompanying statements must be respected.

Sources: [Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006 Annex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=OJ:L:2006:404:0009:0025:En:PDF); [retained FIC Annex XIII, NRVs](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/1169/annex/XIII/data.xht); [GB NHC register](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/great-britain-nutrition-and-health-claims-nhc-register).

**The magnesium row is the commercially important one.** A "full-spectrum mineral blend" of 70 mg total cannot contain 56.25 mg of magnesium and anything else meaningful. **Magnesium claims — tiredness and fatigue, muscle function, nervous system, psychological function — are the ones a hydration/recovery brand most wants, and they are the most expensive to buy in mass terms.** Any magnesium-led claim requires ~56–113 mg of *elemental* magnesium per serving, which at a typical 12% elemental magnesium bisglycinate is **470–940 mg of raw material** — i.e. it consumes the whole active budget under A-019 and most of it even under the revised 4.5 g/30% envelope.

## 4. Botanicals — the on-hold position

### 4.1 What "on hold" means in GB, precisely

- ~2,000 Article 13(1) claims, largely botanical, were never assessed by EFSA. They sit on a DHSC-published list and are neither authorised nor rejected.
- **In GB, the pre-Brexit position is preserved: on-hold claims may continue to be used under the transitional scheme**, provided an application was made before 19 January 2008. DHSC publishes the list and the conditions. The UK government and devolved administrations are "currently developing the next steps".
- **A claim is only an on-hold claim if it matches the spreadsheet entry on all of: the same plant part(s), the same wording, the same health relationship, and the same proposed conditions of use including dose.** Any deviation and it is simply an unauthorised health claim.
- The claim must still comply with Articles 3, 5, 6, 7 and 10 — meaning **the marketer must hold generally accepted scientific evidence substantiating it**, must not mislead, and must carry the mandatory accompanying statements (varied diet, quantity needed for the effect, etc.).
- The ASA polices this actively and has upheld rulings where marketers relied on unsubstantiated botanical language.

Sources: [ASA/CAP Food: Health claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/food-health-claims.html); [DH bulletin on searching the Article 13.1 on-hold spreadsheet](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7eee4de5274a2e87db2cd0/DH_BULLETIN_-_Searching_Article_13.1_on_hold_health_claims_acc.pdf); [Mills & Reeve](https://www.mills-reeve.com/publications/on-hold-botanical-claims-eu-challenge/).

### 4.2 The EU has just closed this door, and GB divergence is temporary

In **Case C-386/23 *Novel Nutriology* (30 April 2025)** the CJEU held that health claims on botanical substances **cannot** be used without prior authorisation despite the Commission's indefinite suspension of the assessment, and was particularly firm about claims referring to **psychological or behavioural functions** — which is exactly the territory a Mind/focus range wants.

Two consequences for V-Drop:
1. The CJEU judgment is not binding in GB, but it is highly persuasive and will be cited by any complainant, enforcement officer or competitor.
2. **The UK and EU agreed in May 2025 to re-align on nutrition and health claims, expected to take effect in mid-2027.** A brand launching a botanical-claims position in 2026–27 is building on a legal basis that is scheduled to change.

Sources: [Cambridge/EJRR case note](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-journal-of-risk-regulation/article/health-claims-on-botanical-substances-prohibited-pending-commission-evaluation-case-c38623-novel-nutriology-2025-eclieuc2025304/A905507541D9DB8FA629BA9FF103C903); [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/european-court-of-justice-restricts-advertising-for-food-with-botanicals); [Vitafoods Insights on GB/EU realignment](https://www.vitafoodsinsights.com/regulations/on-hold-botanical-claims-inch-closer-to-full-assessment-by-the-eu).

## 6. Gender-targeted claims

### 6.1 Can you market "for men" / "for women" in GB?

**Yes.** The ASA considered a complaint that Vitabiotics' advertising implied proven benefits from supplements structured specifically for men and women and **did not uphold it**: it treated the brand names *Wellman* and *Wellwoman* and the phrase "tailored vitamins for men and women" as **non-specific health claims** directed at men and women **as part of the general healthy population**, and therefore compliant. Source: [NutraIngredients on the ASA rulings](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2014/10/29/ASA-pulls-2-Vitabiotics-ads-in-UK/); [Natural Products](https://www.naturalproductsonline.co.uk/news/vitabiotics-ads-come-under-asa-scrutiny-after-health-claims-complaint/).

**What that permits:**
- Branding and naming by sex ("V-Drop for Men").
- Formulating to sex-differentiated nutrient profiles.
- **Non-specific** general wellbeing language ("tailored to men"), **provided** it is accompanied by at least one specific authorised claim from the register — Article 10(3) requires that general, non-specific wellbeing references be accompanied by a specific authorised claim.

**What that does not permit:**
- Claiming the *formulation* is clinically proven to be better for men/women than a generic one, unless you hold the evidence and the claim is authorised.
- Any claim implying treatment of a sex-specific condition (menopause symptoms, erectile function, PMS, prostate health, heavy periods, fertility treatment). These are **medicinal claims** and cross out of food law into the Human Medicines Regulations 2012 — the MHRA, not the FSA, becomes the regulator. This is the sharpest risk in the whole restructure.

### 6.2 The sex-linked authorised claims

**Male-leaning** (all conditioned only on "source of", i.e. 15% NRV — none require a heroic dose):
- "Zinc contributes to the maintenance of normal testosterone levels in the blood" `[V]` — ≥1.5 mg zinc
- "Zinc contributes to normal fertility and reproduction" `[V]` — ≥1.5 mg zinc
- "Selenium contributes to normal spermatogenesis" `[K]` — ≥8.25 µg selenium
- "Zinc contributes to normal DNA synthesis" `[K]`
- Supporting: B-vitamin energy/fatigue claims, magnesium muscle function, vitamin D muscle function.

**Female-leaning:**
- "Folate contributes to maternal tissue growth during pregnancy" `[V]` — ≥30 µg folate. **Conditions restrict the target group to pregnant women.**
- Art. 14 disease-risk reduction: "Supplemental folic acid intake increases maternal folate status. Low maternal folate status is a risk factor in the development of neural tube defects in the developing foetus." `[K]` — conditions require **400 µg/day supplemental folic acid for at least one month before and three months after conception**, and a mandatory accompanying statement. **This is an Article 14 claim: heavier evidentiary and labelling obligations, and it converts the SKU into a pre-conception product with all the caution that implies.**
- "Iron contributes to normal formation of red blood cells and haemoglobin" `[V]` — ≥2.1 mg iron
- "Iron contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue" `[K]`
- Supporting: vitamin C increases iron absorption (a genuinely useful pairing claim); calcium/vitamin D bone claims; biotin hair/skin claims.

**Medicinal-claim red lines for a gendered range:** menopause, hot flushes, "hormone balance", PMS, prostate, libido, erectile function, sperm count as an outcome, "boosts testosterone", "fertility treatment", period pain, "supports conception". **"Zinc contributes to the maintenance of normal testosterone levels in the blood" is authorised; "boosts testosterone" is not — the authorised claim is about maintaining normal levels, not raising them.** That distinction will be the single most tempting line for a male SKU to cross.

### 6.3 Iron in a female SKU — a specific warning

Iron is the obvious female-targeted nutrient and carries the best claims. But iron is also the leading cause of accidental paediatric poisoning from supplements, iron-containing products attract specific warning requirements, and iron in an **effervescent drink** raises palatability and staining issues. **Do not put iron into a gendered SKU without a specific consultant sign-off on warning copy and child-resistant packaging.** UNVERIFIED whether a statutory GB warning threshold applies to iron in food supplements — the consultant must confirm.

## 7. The specification defects (A-020, A-021)

### 7.1 "Methyl B6 & B12: 10 mg" (Mind) — unassessable

One figure for two nutrients whose NRVs differ by ~560×. Readings and what each permits:

| Reading | B6 | B12 | Claims available | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) 10 mg B6 + a trace of B12 | 10 mg = 714% NRV | unknown | All 10 B6 claims | **At the COT 10 mg/day ceiling. Zero headroom. Not recommended.** |
| (b) 10 mg combined, e.g. 9.9 mg B6 + 0.1 mg B12 | 707% NRV | 100 µg = 4,000% NRV | All B6 + all B12 claims | Same B6 problem; B12 has no UL and is tolerated, but 4,000% NRV invites "why?" |
| (c) 10 mg of a premix containing small amounts of both | e.g. 1.4 mg | e.g. 2.5 µg | All B6 + all B12 claims at 100% NRV | **Recommended.** Full claim set, no safety issue, no credibility issue. |

**Reading (c) delivers exactly the same claim set as reading (a) at 1/7th of the B6.** There is no claims-based reason to dose B6 above ~2 mg. Say so to the proposition-architect: the high number buys nothing legally and costs safety margin.

### 7.2 "Vitamin B-Complex (B3, B6, B9, B12)" (Energy) — no doses at all

**No claim can be made, checked or approved until each of the four is given a figure.** Minimums to unlock the full claim set for each: B3 ≥2.4 mg, B6 ≥0.21 mg, B9 ≥30 µg, B12 ≥0.375 µg. These are trivially cheap. **Specifying them at ~100% NRV (B3 16 mg, B6 1.4 mg, B9 200 µg, B12 2.5 µg) costs almost nothing in mass or money and unlocks roughly 32 authorised claims** — including four independent routes to "reduction of tiredness and fatigue" and three to "normal psychological function". **This is the cheapest claim win available to the project.**

### 7.3 "Trace Minerals: 100 mg" / "Full-Spectrum Minerals: 70 mg"

Names no species, so: no permitted-form check, no %NRV declaration, no claim. Under the Food Supplements Regs each vitamin/mineral must be **named and quantified** on the label — a blend declaration of this kind is not compliant as written. And per §3.2, **magnesium at any commercially normal inclusion inside a 70–100 mg blend cannot reach the 56.25 mg threshold**, so the magnesium claims — the ones a recovery/hydration product most wants — are unavailable.

### 7.4 Zinc bisglycinate 10 mg — the two readings

| Reading | Elemental zinc | %NRV | Claims | "High in zinc"? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 mg of the chelate (~20% elemental) | ~2 mg | 20% | **All 18 zinc claims available** | 🔴 No (needs 3 mg) |
| 10 mg elemental zinc | 10 mg | 100% | All 18 | 🟢 Yes |

**So the dose ambiguity costs one nutrition claim, not the claim set.** The serious issue is §2.12: whether zinc bisglycinate is a permitted GB source form at all.

## 8. Labelling must-haves

### 8.1 Food Supplements (England) Regulations 2003 — mandatory particulars `[V]`

Every pack must carry:
1. The **name of the categories** of nutrients or substances characterising the product, or an indication of their nature.
2. The **portion of the product recommended for daily consumption**.
3. A **warning not to exceed the stated recommended daily dose**.
4. A statement that food supplements **should not be used as a substitute for a varied diet**.
5. A statement that the product **should be stored out of the reach of young children**.
6. The **amount of each vitamin/mineral/other substance with a nutritional or physiological effect**, expressed per the recommended daily portion, with **%NRV** for vitamins and minerals (and a note that the NRV figure may be given graphically).
7. The product must **not be presented as preventing, treating or curing disease**, nor refer to such properties.

Sources: [Food Supplements (England) Regulations 2003](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/1387/made); [FSA food supplements guidance](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/food-supplements); [DH labelling summary](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7c8d8440f0b626628acded/Supplements_Labelling_Summary_DH_FINAL.pdf).

### 8.2 Caffeine — this is where V-Drop's Energy SKU gets complicated (A-012)

V-Drop Energy is **both** a food supplement **and** a beverage-when-reconstituted. Two regimes potentially bite:

**(a) Food supplement route — retained FIC Annex III, point 4.2** `[V in substance]`
Required where caffeine is added for a physiological purpose:
> **"Contains caffeine. Not recommended for children or pregnant women"**
in the **same field of vision as the name of the food**, followed **in brackets** by the caffeine content expressed in **mg per portion as recommended for daily consumption**.

**(b) Beverage route — retained FIC Annex III, point 4.1** `[V in substance]`
For beverages (other than coffee/tea-based) intended for consumption without modification **and for beverages in concentrated or dried form which after reconstitution** exceed **150 mg/l** caffeine:
> **"High caffeine content. Not recommended for children or pregnant or breast-feeding women"**
in the same field of vision as the name, followed in brackets by the caffeine content in **mg per 100 ml**.

**Which applies is a legal judgement I cannot make.** The conservative and recommended position is **to carry the "High caffeine content" statement and the per-100 ml declaration**, because V-Drop Energy at 80 mg/500 ml = **160 mg/l as prepared** clears the 4.1 threshold and because the under-16 restriction (§9.3) is drafted to the same criterion.

**Key interpretive point, which answers the CSO's question:** the general note to Annex III provides that the level applies to the product **as proposed ready for consumption or as reconstituted according to the manufacturer's instructions**. **The threshold is therefore measured on the drink as prepared per the label, not as actually consumed.** Source: [retained FIC Annex III](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/1169/annex/III); [lexparency text of Annex III](https://lexparency.org/eu/32011R1169/ANX_III/); [Finnish Food Authority guidance](https://www.ruokavirasto.fi/en/foodstuffs/food-sector/food-information/labelling/instructions-for-use-and-warning/warning-and-instructions-for-use-of-foods-containing-caffeine/).

**Also required by FSA/FSS 2024 guidance for caffeine supplements:** clear and accurate caffeine content, clear dosage instructions, appropriate warnings for children and pregnant women, and accurate measuring devices where relevant.

### 8.3 Green tea / EGCG

If green tea extract is the caffeine source and GB has adopted an equivalent to Reg (EU) 2022/2340 (**UNVERIFIED**), the label must declare EGCG per portion and the maximum number of daily portions, plus warning statements covering the 800 mg cap, cumulative green-tea consumption, avoidance by pregnant/lactating women and under-18s, and not taking on an empty stomach. **Four extra warning statements on a 23 mm tablet's pack is a design problem. Resolve the caffeine source before pack design starts.**

### 8.4 Sweeteners

- Steviol glycosides must be declared in the ingredients list as a sweetener with its name or E number (E 960 / E 960c).
- **"With sweetener(s)"** must accompany the name of the food where the product contains a permitted sweetener. `[K — confirm the exact trigger and wording]`
- Monk fruit: not usable (§2.16).

### 8.5 Allergens

- **Alpha-GPC as specified is derived from SOYA phospholipids (lecithin)** — soya is a named allergen requiring emphasis in the ingredients list. Even if Alpha-GPC is removed, check the origin of any lecithin, flow agent or flavour carrier.
- Spray-dried fruit powders and flavour systems commonly use carriers (maltodextrin, gum acacia, milk derivatives) — obtain full allergen declarations from the contract manufacturer.
- Green tea / caffeine: not an allergen, but **"not suitable for" statements** may be commercially wise.

### 8.6 Other mandatory / recommended statements

| Statement | Trigger |
|---|---|
| "Not recommended for children or pregnant women" (or the "High caffeine content…" variant) | Any added caffeine (§8.2) |
| "Not suitable for children under 16" | **Recommended voluntarily** for the Energy SKU regardless, given §9.3 |
| Sodium content | Effervescent bases are sodium-heavy; must be declared and will be commercially awkward |
| "Do not exceed the stated recommended daily dose" | Always |
| "Store out of the reach of young children" | Always |
| "Food supplements should not be used as a substitute for a varied diet" | Always |
| Vitamin B6 advisory | **Recommended voluntarily** if B6 is above ~2 mg |
| Article 10(2) mandatory accompanying statements | Whenever any health claim is made: importance of a varied, balanced diet and healthy lifestyle; quantity of food and pattern of consumption needed to obtain the effect; where appropriate, a warning for those who should avoid it |

**Article 10(2) is routinely forgotten and is the most common compliance failure in this category.** Every health claim on pack or in an ad drags these statements with it.

| **"Clean energy"** | 🔴 **HIGH** | Two problems at once: (i) "energy" as a physiological benefit is a health claim, and the only authorised route is "contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism" / "reduction of tiredness and fatigue"; (ii) **"clean" is an implied comparative disparagement** of competitor products and invites a CAP misleadingness challenge. | **"contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue"** (B2, B3, B5, B6, B9, B12, C, magnesium, iron). For the "clean" idea, use factual composition: "no artificial sweeteners", "no artificial colours" — each of which must be true and substantiable. |
| **"Boost" / "boosts"** | 🔴 **HIGH** | Implies enhancement above normal. Register claims are almost all about *contributing to normal* function or *maintaining* it. "Boosts immunity" and "boosts testosterone" are the two most-enforced examples. | "contributes to the normal function of the immune system"; "contributes to the maintenance of normal testosterone levels in the blood". |
| **"Immunity" / "immune-boosting"** | 🟠 / 🔴 | **"Immunity" alone is a non-specific claim and needs an accompanying authorised claim (Art. 10(3)).** "Immune-boosting" is 🔴. The authorised territory is real and generous: vitamin C, vitamin D, zinc, B6, B12, folate, iron, copper, selenium all carry immune-system claims. | **"Vitamin C contributes to the normal function of the immune system"** and its siblings. Use "immune support" only alongside one of these. |
| **"Recovery"** | 🟠 **MEDIUM** | No authorised claim uses the word, and in a sports context it implies a physiological benefit. As a **range name** it is defensible if the pack carries authorised claims and does not dress "recovery" as the claim. | Range name: acceptable with care. Benefit copy: **"Magnesium contributes to normal muscle function"**, **"Vitamin C contributes to the maintenance of the normal function of the immune system during and after intense physical exercise"** (at the 200 mg condition) — the latter is the strongest genuinely sports-adjacent authorised line available. |
| **"Clinical dose" / "clinically proven"** | 🔴 **HIGH** | Two distinct breaches: (i) implies efficacy beyond the authorised claim; (ii) CAP Code prohibits health claims referring to the recommendation of an individual health professional, and the ASA has banned Vitabiotics advertising on exactly that ground. Also indefensible for V-Drop given A-019/A-011: lion's mane 200 mg, cordyceps 150 mg, Alpha-GPC 100 mg and ashwagandha 150 mg are all **below** studied ranges. | Drop. If dose transparency is wanted, state the mg and let the consumer judge. |
| **"Bio-performance"** | 🟠 **MEDIUM** | Invented, meaningless, and reads as an implied performance health claim. CAP treats coined terms as claims where consumers would take a health meaning. Also already condemned by customer-insight (§7.4 of `customer-segments.md`): no customer says it. | Retire. Confirms A-016 direction. |
| **"4,000 mg raw equivalent"** | 🔴 **HIGH** | (i) It is an **implied potency claim** with no standard behind it — there is no legal or scientific definition of an extract ratio's "raw equivalent"; (ii) it is **misleading under Art. 3 of 1924/2006 and CAP** because the consumer takes 200 mg, not 4,000 mg; (iii) per A-031 neither V-Drop nor the comparator publishes a beta-glucan marker, so the claim cannot be substantiated; (iv) it is moot if the dual extract fails the novel-food test (§2.5). | **Delete.** Declare the extract mass and, if you want a potency story, the standardised marker content (e.g. % beta-glucans). |
| "Energy" as a SKU name | 🟠 | Product names that convey a health benefit are treated as claims. Permitted **if** accompanied by an authorised claim (Art. 10(3)) — and note that pre-2005 trade marks have a transitional carve-out V-Drop cannot use. | Keep the name; put "Niacin contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue" on the same panel. |
| "Hydration" | 🟠 | No authorised hydration claim exists for any nutrient. | Factual: "electrolytes", "add to 500 ml water". Claim: **"Magnesium contributes to electrolyte balance"**. |
| "Detox", "cortisol", "gut health", "anti-inflammatory", "balances hormones" | 🔴 | All unauthorised; several are medicinal in character. | Avoid. |
| "Natural", "premium", "pocketable", "dissolves clear", "no grit" | 🟢 | Not health claims. Must be factually true. **"Actually dissolves / not gritty" is the strongest ownable phrase customer-insight found and is completely claims-safe.** | Use freely. |

**Structural CAP point:** ASA actively monitors this sector and published six upheld food-supplement rulings on a single day in July 2024. Assume **any** D2C paid social creative will be seen. ### 9.3 The April 2027 under-16 restriction (A-024) — the answer

- **Scope test:** any drink other than tea or coffee containing **more than 150 mg caffeine per litre**, "consistent with the criteria in Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011", **expressly including products intended to be consumed as drinks following reconstitution.**
- **Measurement basis: AS PREPARED ACCORDING TO THE MANUFACTURER'S LABEL INSTRUCTIONS.** This follows from the alignment to the FIC criteria and the FIC Annex III note ("as reconstituted according to the instructions of the manufacturers"). It is **not** measured on what the consumer actually does.
- **Territorial extent: England only**, from **6 April 2027** (subject to Parliament). Scotland, Wales and NI are considering similar measures; the Scottish Government has repeatedly said it does not intend to impose restrictions. **One source (US Library of Congress) captions the item "England and Wales" — I could not reconcile this and mark the exact extent UNVERIFIED.**
- **Channels covered:** convenience stores, online retailers, hospitality, **vending machines**. Online sale requires age verification. Fines up to £2,500, local-authority enforced.
- **V-Drop Energy as briefed: 80 mg / 500 ml = 160 mg/l ⇒ IN SCOPE.**

**Three exits, in order of preference:**

| Option | Arithmetic | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Reduce caffeine to 75 mg per 500 ml** | 150 mg/l exactly — the test is "in excess of 150", so 150 is out of scope | **Cleanest.** Costs 6% of the caffeine. 🟢 |
| **B. Change the label instruction to 600 ml** | 80 mg ÷ 0.6 l = **133 mg/l** — out of scope | **Legally effective on the face of it, but I advise against leading with it.** It is transparently a compliance-by-instruction manoeuvre; it will be read that way by a journalist or a trading standards officer; consumers carry 500 ml bottles; and it does nothing about the FIC 4.1 warning if the drink is still perceived as high-caffeine. 🟠 |
| **C. Accept age-restriction** | — | Under a D2C-only Phase 1 (D-2026-09-21) this means age verification at checkout — friction at exactly the conversion step, plus it forecloses any future retail/vending route. 🔴 for a premium adult wellness brand that has already excluded under-18s as a target. |

**Recommendation: Option A.** 75 mg is within a rounding error of 80 mg functionally, and it removes an entire regulatory workstream, an age-gate, a vending exclusion and a reputational exposure. **This should be a CSO decision this week, because it constrains formulation, pack copy and the D2C checkout build.**

## 10. What the proposition can lean on — one page for `proposition-architect`

### 10.1 Energy

**Strongest defensible territory: "tiredness and fatigue", via B vitamins.**

- 🟢 **"Niacin contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue"** — plus the same claim independently from B2, B5, B6, B9, B12 and vitamin C. Six independent routes to one message.
- 🟢 "Vitamin B12 contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism"; "Vitamin B12 contributes to normal psychological function".
- 🟢 "Pantothenic acid contributes to normal mental performance" — **if B5 is added.** It is not currently in the Energy formulation and it should be. It is the nearest legal approach to "focus" in the entire register, and it costs pennies.
- 🔴 Caffeine and L-theanine carry **nothing**. They can be declared and quantified, and that is all. **The Energy SKU's entire claim story must be carried by vitamins that are not currently dosed (A-021).**
- **Action:** dose B3/B5/B6/B9/B12 at ~100% NRV; cut caffeine to 75 mg/500 ml; specify the caffeine source and EGCG.

**One-line proposition territory:** *fizzy B-vitamin energy that helps reduce tiredness and fatigue, with 75 mg of caffeine, and it actually dissolves.*

### 10.2 Mind

**Weakest of the three. It has no legal ground to stand on as briefed.**

- 🔴 Alpha-GPC: unauthorised novel food. 🔴 Cordyceps: FSA stop-sale. 🟠🔴 Lion's mane dual extract: probably novel. 🔴 L-theanine: no claims, possibly novel. 🔴 Creatine at 1,500 mg: no claim; and the cognitive claim was **refused**. 🔴 "Nootropic": an ASA-ruled specific health claim.
- **Three of the four actives are legally unusable and the fourth can say nothing.**
- The only rescue is a **vitamin-led "mental performance" build**: pantothenic acid ("normal mental performance"), plus B3/B6/B7/B9/B12/C/zinc/magnesium ("normal psychological function"), plus iodine/zinc/iron ("normal cognitive function"). That is a legitimate, well-populated claim territory — **but it is a B-complex, not a mushroom product, and it should be named and designed as one.**
- Read with A-026 (78% of Britons have heard little or nothing about functional mushrooms; 3% trial) and A-034 (taste-masking mass penalty), **the regulatory evidence now independently supports dropping or radically re-basing Mind.** Three separate workstreams have reached the same conclusion from different directions.

**One-line proposition territory:** *if it survives — a daily B-complex that contributes to normal mental performance and normal psychological function. No mushrooms.*

### 10.3 Recovery / Wellness (and the proposed vitamin-led split)

**Strongest of the three, and it gets stronger under the restructure.**

- 🟢 **Vitamin C at 200 mg = 250% NRV** unlocks 14 claims, "high in vitamin C", and — subject to the conditions read — **"Vitamin C contributes to the maintenance of the normal function of the immune system during and after intense physical exercise"**, which is the only sports-adjacent authorised claim in the brief's whole ingredient list.
- 🟢 **Vitamin D3 at 25 µg** unlocks 7 claims including immune function, muscle function and bones.
- 🟢 **Zinc** unlocks 18 claims from just 1.5 mg — including immune function, cognitive function, and (for a male SKU) testosterone and fertility.
- 🟠 **Magnesium** is the claim the category wants and the hardest to afford: ≥56.25 mg elemental. Decide deliberately whether to buy it.
- 🟠 **Ashwagandha** is a live FSA/COT safety review, has no authorised claim, and is under-dosed. **Remove it.** Its removal simultaneously frees the active budget for magnesium and resolves A-011.
- **Everyday / Men / Women split:** supported. Men's differentiators = zinc (testosterone, fertility, DNA synthesis) + selenium (spermatogenesis). Women's = iron (red blood cells & haemoglobin, tiredness) + folate (maternal tissue growth; and, if you dare, the Art. 14 neural-tube claim at 400 µg) + vitamin C (increases iron absorption). Everyday = the B-complex/C/D/zinc core.

**One-line proposition territory:** *the immune, energy and muscle claims people actually recognise — vitamin C, D and zinc — in a drink that dissolves clear, in a format they'll carry.*

### 10.4 The uncomfortable strategic truth to put to the CSO

The vitamin-led restructure **is** the right call on claims. But the reason it is right — that these claims are authorised, cheap to satisfy, and available from commodity nutrients at 15% NRV — is **exactly** the reason every competitor already has them. Tesco's £0.06/serving effervescent vitamin C can say all 14 vitamin C claims. **A vitamin-led V-Drop wins the right to speak, not the right to charge £0.83.** The premium must be argued on format, taste, ritual, design and subscription convenience — none of which is a claim, and all of which are unregulated and ownable.
