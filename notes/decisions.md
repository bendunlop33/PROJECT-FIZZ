| 2026-09-22 | **The test is EFFICACY: a product that truly works and delivers what you need — not a product others don't have.** | User correction, the third and deepest of the session, and it **overturns a design rule the CSO had asserted twice**. **What it undoes:** the CSO repeatedly argued that combination claims are "free" because each needs only **15% NRV**, which sits below the taste ceiling. **On an efficacy test that is the wrong target.** 15% NRV is the legal minimum at which a nutrient may be named in a claim; it is not a dose that corrects a shortfall. A product designed to 15% is designed to be *sayable*, which is precisely the criticism this project levelled at Waterdrop. **The efficacy target is ~100% NRV of the nutrients people are actually short of — not 15%, which does nothing, and not 1,000%, which is excreted.** **Selection basis changes from market scarcity to evidenced UK shortfall.** Worked against NDNS Years 1–9 §3.4, read directly (`research/efficacy-design-basis.md`). Real adult shortfalls: **vitamin D — mean intakes below the RNI in ALL age/sex groups, the only universal one; magnesium — low intakes across all adults; iodine — worsening; folate — worsening in women 19–64; iron — worsening**; selenium probably large but on a secondary source. **Where scarcity and need diverge, need wins — and one of the CSO's own recommendations falls: riboflavin.** NDNS finds mean riboflavin intake **above the RNI in every adult group**. It was pushed as whitespace on scarcity grounds; on an efficacy test it is low priority. Vitamin A and manganese are likewise scarce but not needed — they earn a place only to complete a *claim* set, which is the wrong reason. **The format mostly permits it.** At full 100% NRV, D + iodine + selenium + folate + iron + C + zinc is roughly **230 mg of actives — comfortable inside a 2.0 g cube**. **The one real failure is magnesium:** a genuine all-adult shortfall that no cube can meaningfully dose (375 mg NRV ≈ 1,900 mg as citrate; the whole category tops out at 56 mg). **A cube can be a source of magnesium; it cannot solve a magnesium shortfall, and the brand must not imply otherwise.** **Two unused efficacy levers, both legal and both invisible on a front-of-pack number:** bioavailable source forms — A-043 confirmed Schedule 2 permits the bisglycinates, methylcobalamin, P-5-P and calcium-L-methylfolate, while the category declares zinc oxide, cyanocobalamin, ferrous lactate and pyridoxine hydrochloride — and real absorption pairing. **And the claims follow rather than lead:** the efficacy core happens to carry the complete authorised immune set bar copper, the complete cognitive set, the iron-absorption pair and the tiredness claim on four nutrients. **Design for efficacy and the claims come free; design for claims and you get a 15% NRV product that says a lot and does little.** **Unchanged honest gap: nobody asked for this.** The review data shows no demand for combinations or higher dosing; the largest complaint remains flavour intensity. | Supersedes the 15% NRV design target used in the combination and range-architecture rows below; those rows' claim analysis stands, their dosing guidance does not |
